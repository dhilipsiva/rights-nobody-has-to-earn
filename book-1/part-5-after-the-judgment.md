# After the Judgment

*A documented case, told from reporting on what followed the judgment that
opens Part I.*

The families evicted from Wallacedene in the winter of 1999 took their case to
South Africa's Constitutional Court under the name of one of them, Irene
Grootboom. The judgment of October 2000, written by Justice Zak Yacoob,
declared what the state must do. The question of temporary housing for that
community had already been settled between the government and the community on
the second day of argument, as Yacoob later recalled. The community received
building materials, a patch of land on which to build and basic services, and
its members were assigned to phase four of a ten-phase housing
development.[^new-frame]

Irene Grootboom died in her self-built shack on the temporary relocation site
in 2008, almost eight years after the judgment. The reports place her death at
the end of July or in the first days of August. The *Mail & Guardian* reported
that she died still homeless and penniless, and quoted her lawyer, Ismail
Jamie: "the fact that she died homeless shows how the legal system and civil
society failed her".[^mg] Mawethu Sila, a community leader with her at the
time, said in 2020 that he and most of the others in the case had received
state housing by 2011. The house she would have lived in was built after her
death, and members of her family live in it.[^new-frame]

What failed is disputed. Yacoob put it down to corruption, greed, selfishness
and lack of attention to poor people, which he said the Court could not have
foreseen.[^new-frame] Jamie said he was sorry that they had not done enough to
follow the case up after judgment was given in her favour.[^mg] Neither account
is tested here.

Part V draws the book's arguments together and ranks where the design could
fail. A right declared and a house built years later are different facts, and
the distance between them is the measure this Part holds the design to. A
constitution can name who owes the house, what evidence would show it arrived
and who must act when it does not. Whether anyone builds it belongs to the
second book, and it is the first failure the last chapter ranks.

    *New Frame*, 5 October 2020, [republished by Public Interest Legal

    temporary relief, the development phase, Sila's account and the house are
    the article's reporting; Yacoob's account of the settlement and of why the
    judgment failed is his, as the article quotes it.

[^mg]: Pearlie Joubert, ["Grootboom dies homeless and
    penniless"](https://mg.co.za/article/2008-08-08-grootboom-dies-homeless-and-penniless/),
