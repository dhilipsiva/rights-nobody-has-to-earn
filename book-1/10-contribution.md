# Contribution

Esa taught a student. Quin did the census. Gia examined Bela's conduct and
recorded a ground. These are different entries about different acts. None
creates a constitutional status called recognition, and none measures its
subject's worth.

Bela also taught someone: Cira. The record contains a completed adverse finding
against Bela. Both entries remain. The finding does not erase the teaching or
turn an unrelated contribution into fraud.

## What the record says

A teaching entry names a teacher and a learner. An employment entry names a
person and a task. Neither shows how long the activity lasted, how well
it was done, whether it was paid or what its recipient received. Entering a
contribution is not an assessment of the person who made it.

The care cases make those limits visible. Nima is Pico's parent and teaches
Pico. Sata has an employment entry naming care. Neither entry shows that care
arrived, that it was safe, that its recipient consented or that the
caregiver acquired authority over them. Pico acquires no contribution merely
from being taught. When Pico teaches Ona, the record identifies Pico's own act.

Caregiver and dependant keep separate rights. The public duty to maintain care
does not become a family invoice, and a care entry cannot discharge it. A
valuable contribution may also be absent from the record. The absence tells
us what this account can support, not whose life was useful.

## Each claim needs its own ground

The previous chapter distinguishes labour protection, competence certification,
compensation and contribution-based supplements. A teaching or employment entry
cannot substitute for the records each requires. Nor does a wage prove
competence, or a certificate prove that compensation is owed.

In the compensation case, a payer's promise needs an independent authorised
attestation on the matching basis. The supplement case needs a contribution
history and an independently adjudicated event under the same scheme. These
claims identify what is owed and why; they do not confer a general rank.
Their conclusions are not evidence of payment.

A contribution entry has no age premise. An employment entry could be
recorded for Cira without any recorded evidence of general adulthood. That
would settle neither Cira's age nor permission to employ a child. Child
activity must remain voluntary, safe, compatible with education and protected
by the applicable labour and pay requirements. Recording the activity shows
none of those conditions to be met.

## An examination is not authority

Gia, one of the examiners in Bela's credibility case, has an examination and
its stated ground in the record. Suppose Yano records a judgment and
examination about Adam, then cites as the ground nothing more than a hunch.
Giving Yano a ground to cite does not confer the authority to sign findings,
prove the ground true or complete an adverse case.

Esa's parent records a judgment about Esa. That relationship bars the
proposed adverse finding; it does not turn the examination into a finding
against the parent. A separate case must prove any concealed conflict. Tove
gives another contrast. Tove has an examination in the record and has been
recalled; suppose Tove also cites a ground for that examination. The recall
blocks Tove's authority to sign findings, while the examination and its ground
remain recorded. An examination entry cannot restore the recalled authority.

An auditor's pay, if claimed, needs the ordinary compensation record. Merely
examining someone creates no constitutional acknowledgment or payment. Whether
the examination supports an adverse conclusion depends on the case, evidence,
procedure and authority described in the later chapters.

## A finding does not cancel a life of work

Bela's adverse case identifies conduct that can be challenged. It prevents Bela
from signing a new credibility finding against somebody else while the case
remains in force. It does not show that Bela never taught Cira, that Cira
learned nothing, or that every other contribution was dishonest.

Suppose, in a separate test, that Bela is also the claimant in a wage case
and an illness case. The test records independent support for each claim and
an unrelated care entry, and keeps Bela's personal finding. Both claims
follow. Compensation fraud on another basis does not defeat this wage; a
finding on the wage's own attested basis does. The supplement still follows,
and Bela remains owed food. Correction follows the particular claim's rules.

Cira does not inherit Bela's finding. An employment entry for Cira would be a
separate entry of Cira's own, just as Pico's teaching is separate from the
teaching Pico received. Bela keeps personhood, the ballot and free movement in
this case. No prisoner conclusion follows.

Restoration concerns the finding and the powers it restricts. Recorded
contributions do not depend on it. There is no constitutional recognition
balance to rebuild and no general loss of it to record. Restoration by a
properly made appellate decision, and the continuing review duty, are examined
in the chapters on findings.

## What remains outside these entries

People can praise, criticise, associate and refuse association within the
ordinary protections of expression, equality and other people's rights.
The constitution calculates no distribution of esteem. Neither that absence
nor a formal protection proves that a workplace or community treats someone
fairly.

The distinction to keep is between evidence of an act and authority over a
person. The next chapter asks what money may buy, and where that
permission ends.
