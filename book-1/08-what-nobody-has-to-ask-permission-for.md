# What Nobody Has to Ask Permission For

People may learn, publish, believe or refuse belief, gather, research, make
art, speak their own language, play sport, keep friendships, form associations
and help their neighbours without first obtaining official permission. Public
power bears the burden of justifying any restriction on these activities, and
one person's freedom gives them authority over nobody else's association or
cooperation.

## The things power may not prescribe

The rules conclude ten limits on public power for Adam by name. Each forbids a
kind of act against him: arbitrary interference with his privacy; coercion of
his bodily choices; restriction of his movement without a supported ground; a
decision with consequences for him made without review; forced association;
prescribed expression; compelled conscience; a prescribed form of family; an
unequal civic status; and prescription of his private or civic life.
Personhood is the whole premise of each.

For Nell, whose record holds a birth entry and nothing more, these rules ask
for nothing and forbid a great deal: the same limits follow for her. She is
also owed learning, expression, belief and company, each by the State, and the
limits stand apart from those debts. Learning follows where a teacher teaches
her and she hears; company follows from a matching receipt attested by an
independent witness authorised for her. Expression and belief lie beyond any
provider's power to certify, so the rules stay silent about what she says and
believes. On her one-line record all four remain debts.

The limits bind public power directly and oblige public institutions to
prevent, investigate and answer private interference; a private person owes
another a direct duty only where a rule names one. A limit on prescribed
expression is a different thing from a recorded voice. Each limit forbids an
act; whether a public body committed one needs its own evidence.

## When a restriction may be made

A public body that wants to narrow one of these activities must first have a
reviewed restriction in force in one of ten named fields, which together cover
the activities above; a record naming any other field completes nothing. The
ground must be an evidenced harm to someone's rights or to the commons, and
those are the only two; the record names offence, disagreement and an official
view of the truth as outside it. The record must also show that the least
restrictive means that would work was considered. The restriction must define
the activity it reaches and the people it affects, give public reasons with
accessible notice, provide independent review with a challenge that suspends it
during that review, stay neutral as to viewpoint, belief and identity, leave
activity beyond its reach free of prior permission, certify nothing about
truth, taste or creative merit, and name its end. It restricts conduct or sets
a condition on access.

Three separate attesters must record every element identically: a source, an
independent evidence authority and an independent reviewer, each distinct from
the restricting body. If two of them disagree on an element that takes one
value, the restriction stays incomplete. A responding office hears challenges,
with an independent alternate behind it; both stand apart from the attesters,
from each other and from the restricting body. While the record is current,
the restricting body holds authority for that restriction and must publish its
reasons and evidence, and the responding office must hear challenges and
correct the record. A superseded record confers nothing.

Enforcement is a separate record that must name the reviewed restriction and
match it on matter, field, restricting body, version, period, jurisdiction,
legal scope, end and form. A restriction therefore reaches only its own matter
and field: one reviewed for a newspaper leaves a gallery untouched. Enforcement
must be proportionate to the reviewed harm alone and must give the affected
person notice, reasons and an accessible challenge before it takes effect.
Standing, the floor and the ballot lie outside its reach.

## When the ground was missing

The rules follow one sequence through. A restriction in learning and
information access completes on an evidenced harm to rights, and a record
enforcing it against one person completes as well. Three separate attesters
then complete a defect record finding the harm ground unevidenced. The
restriction's authority ends, and with it the enforcement permission and every
duty resting on the two. The responding office named on the defect must review
and correct the record, and the restricting body must publish the correction. A
defect against some other record leaves this restriction in force.

Throughout, the person the restriction reached remains a person, owed food and
holding the ballot, with the limits against compelled conscience and coerced
association intact. The rules conclude nothing adverse against them: a
credibility finding, confinement and a recorded loss all stay absent.

## Freedom written down

The ordinary case runs the other way. A residual record can state, for a named
activity, person and public body, that no reviewed restriction applies, that no
registration, licence or approval conditions the activity, that no register of
belief, opinion or association membership is kept, and that nothing on the
record certifies truth, taste or creative merit. It then binds that body to
leave the activity free of any permission requirement and to refrain from
registering what people believe, what opinions they hold and what they have
joined. It grants the body nothing: authority to restrict and permission to
enforce stay absent.

Because this record only protects, its duties follow from its source's record
alone, and the independent reviewer named on it owes prompt review and can
withdraw them. Records of public information, accessible communication and the
independence of inquiry and creation work the same way; a restriction, its
enforcement, a defect finding and a concentration finding wait for all three
attesters.

A record that merely notes someone's belief or association concludes nothing
about them. They remain a person, owed food and holding the ballot, and keep
the limits against compelled conscience, coerced association and involuntary
association.

## Information, inquiry and concentration

A reviewed public-information record lets a requester see, correct or receive
in accessible form information held by or for a public function, and obliges
the holding body to supply it accessibly. A refusal needs a reviewed ground and
reasons, with any releasable part released; the requester need give no reason
for asking, and no fee may shut them out. Public bodies must communicate in
accessible formats and plain language, adjust on request free of charge, and
convey every meaning through more than one sense or notation. A researcher or
artist named on a reviewed record may publish or perform without prior
approval; the public body must leave question, method, result and content to
them, and funding conditions stop short of dictating findings.

A concentration finding concerns structure: ownership, distribution, and
ranking and attention. It obliges the concentrated body to disclose its reach
and the basis of its ranking, and lets the responding office open an
independent plurality review. It judges no truth or editorial choice,
classifies no audience or belief, and restricts nothing.

## Who bears the cost

A restricting body must assemble evidence, three agreeing attesters, reasons
and review before it acts, and the people a restriction would protect wait
while it does; while a challenge suspends the restriction, they wait again. A
restriction needs one of the two grounds, so people hurt only by offence or
disagreement find this route closed. A protective record binds a body on one
source's word, before any review, and the body bears those duties unless a
reviewer withdraws them.

## What this cannot settle

The harm ground is whatever three separate attesters record. If they agree in
calling offence a harm to rights, the restriction completes as surely as an
honest one would; the challenge, the defect route and final review are where
that relabelling can be caught.

The duty to leave an activity free of permission, and to keep belief and
membership unregistered, binds a body only through a residual record naming
that activity. Without one, the ten limits every person holds still apply.

A defect finding corrects the record; a remedy for the person a restriction
reached needs its own route. Whether any office publishes, hears, corrects or
holds back is a question for the second book.

## Argument: Two grounds and an end

*The companion states these rules as [Article 7](https://dhilipsiva.dev/rights-nobody-has-to-earn/constitution/#article-7).*

I choose to put the whole case for a restriction on the public body, and to
write the freedom that remains as duties on a named body, because a liberty
that lasts only until an official objects is held on sufferance. Freedom read
from silence would rest on an absent entry, and the rules treat an absent entry
as silence rather than a finding; a residual record names the body that must
leave an activity free of permission and keep belief and membership
unregistered. The burden itself is borrowed. The Siracusa Principles, drawn up
at an expert conference in 1984 and circulated as a document of the UN
Commission on Human Rights, state that a limit on a Covenant right that must be
"necessary" has to respond to a pressing public or social need, pursue a
legitimate aim and be proportionate, use no more restrictive means than
required, and leave the burden of justification with the State.[^siracusa]

The strongest alternative keeps the burden and the test of means but leaves the
purpose open to argument in each case. Aharon Barak treats proportionality as
the main tool for limiting constitutional rights and analyses it in four
components: a proper purpose, a rational connection to it, necessity, meaning
that no less restrictive means would achieve the purpose, and a final stage of
balancing.[^barak] Its strength is that the question of purpose is put to each
restriction as it comes, so a reviewing court can weigh an aim that a
list-maker failed to foresee, and the balancing stage still guards the right.

I answer the purpose question in advance, with two grounds, and that costs
whatever an open test would have protected. People whose injury from speech is
offence alone, a community whose sacred things are mocked among them, find the
restriction route closed, where a balancing court might weigh their injury
against the speaker's freedom. I accept that cost because I would rather keep
offence, disagreement and an official view of the truth off the scale
altogether than trust each balance to weigh them lightly against a dissenter.
The suspensive challenge adds a second cost: while a challenge runs, the
restriction waits, and the people it would protect bear the harm meanwhile. The
written record adds a third, smaller one: a body's particular duties wait for
someone to make it, though the ten limits hold in the meantime. I require an
end because a restriction justified by one harm at one time should lapse with
its reasons unless it is justified again.

I would add a third ground if a serious, evidenced injury kept recurring that
neither ground could name. I would reopen the list only on evidence that an
open purpose test, applied by independent reviewers, protects dissent and
unpopular belief as reliably as two fixed grounds while answering those
injuries too. And I would narrow the suspensive challenge on evidence that
challenges routinely keep evidenced harms running through review.
