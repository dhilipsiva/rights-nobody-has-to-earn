# The Vote Conviction Does Not Take

In Hano's case the rules conclude confinement and a ballot entitlement
together. Under the custody rules Hano loses free movement and remains part of
the electorate.

Here, conviction and custody take neither franchise nor candidacy. Political
rights have their own basis, which punishment status leaves untouched. The basic
ballot entitlement follows from personhood and recorded general adulthood. Hano
has both. Adulthood itself attaches in law at the common age; a registrar
records evidence and cannot grant or withhold the status.

The full political framework also requires ordinary residence and one chosen
political home at each applicable tier. Citizenship, property, a conventional
address, immigration classification, employment, wealth, document perfection or
a waiting period cannot replace that baseline. Registration records membership
rather than creating it. Residence and a valid public result each need more
than the basic ballot conclusion.

An enterprise receives no ballot, candidacy, human standing, or extra political
weight. It may petition, testify and publish attributed views, but its treasury
cannot fund candidates or parties or purchase independent electoral advocacy.
Public, private, cooperative, mutual and nonprofit enterprise forms face the
same prohibition. Their members keep individual political rights. The rules
for unions and voluntary civic associations cannot be settled merely by
calling them enterprises.

## What conviction leaves intact

The conviction mechanism withdraws free movement while preserving the person's
other rights. Hano remains a person, is owed the floor including expression, and
retains the ballot conclusion. No credibility finding follows for Hano in this
case, and custody produces no recorded act of expression.

The retained franchise matters because people subject to confinement remain
entitled to participate in choosing the government responsible for it.
Speech, challenge and remedy remain separate rights and separate ways to
object. Conviction does not convert political membership into a reward for
conduct the State approves.

## A credibility finding does not remove the vote

Bela is under a credibility finding and still holds the ballot conclusion. The
finding bars its subject's signature on a new credibility finding and leaves
citizenship, residence and adulthood where they were. It says nothing about
Bela's capacity for political judgment.

Hano and Bela test different routes to the same boundary. A custody conclusion
and a credibility finding each coexist with the franchise. Neither verdict
gives a reason to subtract political weight.

## The home custody does not move

Confinement creates no new political home and removes no old one. A prison,
detention site, institution, shelter, eviction destination, military posting
or other compelled placement cannot replace the last voluntary uncontested
home. The public result must contain positive evidence of that continuity,
which the custody entry alone cannot give.

A voluntary move sets the new political home and ends the old in the same
transition. During a dispute, the last uncontested
voluntary home remains. A first-time resident with no earlier home has a
provisional route through accessible evidence, an attested cross-jurisdiction
omission record and independent review. Silence or a bare assertion of no
earlier home cannot supply it.

A submission already accepted and in force for a decision remains the
person's only one through a move. The new home cannot issue another. A positive
provisional status can preserve an expiring opportunity while review continues,
without creating a second ballot, extending continuity or prolonging an
incumbent's authority.

A nonresident receives no local, regional or common ballot or candidacy at
those tiers. Universal standing, protection, petition and the former resident's
right to return remain. Return creates no diaspora ballot, and citizenship
creates no extra electoral weight.

Candidacy shares the adult-resident baseline. A present incompatibility,
separately proved, can exclude someone from a particular office only if it is
individualised, relevant, proportionate, time-limited and reviewable. It leaves
the person's vote untouched. A later move cannot silently end or extend a
current office.

These legal conditions work from evidence of residence and age. Removing
needed personhood evidence or withholding the age record can prevent the basic
franchise conclusion even where the entitlement exists in law. Esa's case
makes that distinction visible: without adulthood evidence no ballot follows
for Esa, and if that evidence were entered, the ballot would follow.
Challenge, independent determination, continuity and provisional opportunity
address disputed evidence; none of them observes a birthday.

## What protects the entitlement

One test adds a rule granting the ballot to adults who are not prisoners.
The rule is accepted. Hano nevertheless retains the ballot because the broader
entitlement also remains. An additional grant has not repealed the existing
one. Replacing the broader rule would be a different change; losing Hano's
entitlement would fail the prisoner ballot check.

This result concerns these two rules only: elsewhere, a new entry can defeat a
condition that depended on its absence.

An attempted entry simply declaring a ballot entitlement is refused; the
evidence required by the rule must support it. Even an entry purporting to
permit direct declarations leaves that condition in place. When a test submits
a person entry together with a forged entitlement, both are refused. The
[method](method.md#rejecting-a-whole-input) follows this case through its exact
inputs. These refusals concern entitlement. A cast ballot, tally or reported
institutional decision needs separate evidence.

These safeguards answer different attacks. Testing the entitlement under
conviction leaves its rule open to editing; the amendment system governs
proposed changes, and a rule the computer accepts is not thereby a lawful
amendment.

The floor has a separate protection against the tested rule that confines
someone for lacking a floor condition. That refusal answers that attack alone,
and the ballot rule can still be replaced. Each guarantee needs safeguards
suited to how it can be changed.

## A right still needs a ballot

A public result must bind the exact decision, configuration, roster, separate
completeness assurance, unique submissions, legal rule, result, challenge and
correction route, independent certification and current source. That is what
allows a certified result to support public authority.

The rules check the recorded premises and the bounded authority they support.
They do not authenticate the roster, discover an omitted resident, operate a
changing tally or resolve competing submissions. A ballot entitlement with no
accessible ballot is an undelivered right, and whether Hano's vote is collected
belongs to the second book.

## The child with nobody

Nell's birth entry gives no evidence of general adulthood, so no ballot
conclusion follows. The record cannot distinguish being below the threshold
from missing evidence that it has been reached. It shows neither a punishment
nor Nell's actual age.

Nell's right to be heard is already hers: the prohibition on a minimum speaking
age follows from personhood alone. Participation is wider than the franchise.
