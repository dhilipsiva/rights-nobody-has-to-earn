# Why recognition exists, and what it buys

## Item 41 — the residue, 2026-09-24

The chapters no longer list "recognition loss" among the consequences a finding
does not have, since no recognition status exists for a finding to reach. The
word remains where the book explains why the design keeps no such status, where
recognition in the ordinary social sense is a forbidden allocation or priority
key, and where a pinned experiment tests a hostile rule recording such a loss.

## Supersession, 2026-09-20: remove the constitutional badge and blanket loss

Item 21's source and prose are session-drafted, author-approved under delegated
approval (2026-09-13). This ruling supersedes the retention decision below,
including the requirement that recognition have three producers. It changes no
standing, floor, compensation, supplement, certificate, political or custody
rule. The implementation and required checks are complete.

### Finding established against the current source

At `add2bb63`, recognition still had three producers and no consumers. Every
producer read personal `false/1` under negation. The fourth relevant rule read
that same finding to produce `lose(Points, subject)`. Consequently a finding
about one incident suppressed acknowledgment of every recorded contribution,
without establishing that unrelated teaching, employment or care was false.
This is an unjustified normative reach, not a discovered contradiction.

The earlier claim that recognition was the finding's only consequence was no
longer true: the current prospective-signature rule already excluded both
signers with effective personal findings. Reasons and challenge duties also
remained, and the separate incident finding reached the corresponding
exposure's shield. Recognition therefore supplied neither indispensable
accountability nor the only way to distinguish contribution from entitlement.

### Alternatives and resolution

- **Keep the unspendable badge and blanket withdrawal:** rejected. No financial
  consumer makes the formal material effect small, not the social judgment
  proportionate. A person's unrelated acts supply no ground for the penalty.
  Keeping a sanction to give a badge content, and a badge to give the sanction
  content, is circular. No empirical benefit of this particular public badge
  has been established.
- **Keep acknowledgment and remove its penalty:** materially better, but still
  maintains a general public status with no identified legal task. It can
  stigmatise unrecorded contributors and leaves a new public judgment to police.
  Pay, certification and supplements already express their particular grounds.
  Public or private appreciation does not need a constitutional Boolean status.
- **Narrow withdrawal to the disputed contribution:** a reasonable alternative
  where an actual claim relies on that record. The old unary status cannot
  distinguish contributions. A richer record could do so; the language is not
  the constraint. Such records would need act identity, evidence, correction
  and ending. The existing compensation and insurance routes already correct
  their own attested basis or person/scheme. A second public-esteem register
  would duplicate that work without giving the affected person a further remedy.
- **Correct consequential records and restrict relevant official acts:** retained.
  A personal credibility finding restricts the ability to sign a new adverse
  finding. An exposure-specific deceit finding affects that exposure's shield.
  Compensation fraud and contribution fraud must meet their distinct rules;
  neither is inferred from general credibility. A bare activity entry creates
  no delivery, payment, qualification, personal rank or official authority.
- **Remove constitutional recognition and clawback:** chosen. It eliminates the
  unjustified blanket effect and the unsupported public-status mechanism itself.
  It does not claim that contributions cannot be noticed or that informal esteem
  is harmless. Its cost is foregoing a uniform constitutional acknowledgment.
  The reasons to reconsider would be a demonstrated unmet need for that specific
  public status and adequate protection against stigma and indirect hierarchy.

This is a substantive removal, not a defence of a known defect, a claim that
finer correction is impossible, or a transfer of the constitutional issue to
Book 2. Social neglect remains a real concern; labour rights, care duties and
contestable compensation have concrete tasks that the badge did not perform.

### Duration, restoration and nonresponse

An effective personal finding still prevents a new adverse signature. The
qualified case-specific appellate disposition can end it while preserving the
history, and prevents revival under another record identifier. It need not
turn an unrelated carried credential restriction into a valid appointment.

The existing reasons duty and subject-triggered appeal remain. A new duty on
Appeals requires review of the continuing restriction without waiting for a
request. This duty ceases for a restored finding when no other effective
personal finding remains. No duty is a performed review. No expiry or elapsed
period is inferred from silence; the person awaiting review remains restricted
from this particular coercive act. An automatic expiry without an independently
established disposition would let institutional nonresponse reopen power over
somebody else. Ordinary work, wages, political rights and the floor cannot be
held pending that disposition. The restriction's justification is integrity of
that official act, not punishment through public esteem or a judgment that the
person can never recover. The appellate record supplies the ending; institutions
owe the act, and the model cannot establish its outside performance.

## The purpose

A design of this kind meets one question it cannot dodge: what about the people
who contribute more? Two answers fail. Answering with a number builds a
currency, which chapter 10 argues at length. Having no concept of contribution
at all does not answer the question, it changes the subject, and it leaves a
society unable to say that it sees the work done in it.

Recognition is the third answer: the design records that somebody contributed,
and the record confers nothing — not little, not nothing yet, nothing, because
no rule reads it. That is a positive constitutional statement about what
noticing a contribution buys, and it is what makes the floor's unconditionality
legible rather than merely asserted: a society that can see contribution and
still owes the same to everybody has said something a society with no concept of
contribution has not.

## Retain, simplify, or remove — the comparison

**Removing it** was examined and is refused, and the reason is not sentiment.
Recognition is the only thing a credibility finding reaches. Delete it and the
harshest conclusion this design can reach about a person short of confinement
reaches nothing at all — a finding with no content. The voiding chapters, the
clawback chapter and the whole argument about what an adverse finding may and
may not cost would be arguing about an instrument with no effect. The pair holds
each other up: recognition gives the finding something to reach, and the finding
is what keeps recognition from being decoration.

**Keeping it because the slot is occupied** is refused as a reason, though it is
tempting. An occupied slot is not safer than an empty one; somebody who wants to
build a score can write one either way, and what stands against them is
`recognition_is_minted_never_read_and_never_counted` and the floor-vector guards
rather than the furniture. The prose says so rather than claiming the weaker
protection.

**Simplifying the `Points` constant** was examined and declined. `lose(Points, ·)`
names a unit this design does not have — the book never speaks of points as a
currency, and Part V's only use of the word is historical evidence about another
society. Renaming it would touch sixty-one pin sites for a cosmetic gain, and
the rename is self-checking, so it stays available. It is declined here because
chapter 26 already names the thing correctly in prose — "a loss against Bela's
name" — so the constant misleads nobody who reads the book, and churn of that
size belongs to a pass that is already touching those files.

## What the chapters now say

Chapter 10 gains "Why it exists at all", stating the purpose, the measured
absence of readers, the one consequence that runs the other way, and the
objection-and-answer above. Chapter 10's pins run the claim: after the voiding,
Bela keeps personhood, the food debt, the ballot and free movement, and the
student of a voided teacher holds recognition earned through her own work and
loses none of it.

Chapter 26's Cira section states the current boundary rather than a history of
it. The argument for reaching the student is given in full because it is a good
argument, the narrowing is shown to be a repeal, and the cost of the boundary is
stated: some recognition somewhere rests on work that was not done, and the
design leaves it there rather than hold an instrument that can reach a person
who did nothing.

Chapter 9 keeps recognition distinct from a wage, a certificate, a contribution
history and an above-floor supplement, each concluded from its own record and
read by nothing else.

## What does not follow

Nothing here makes recognition an entitlement, a payment, an allocation key, a
qualification, a franchise weight or an indirect condition of the floor, and
nothing reads it. Nobody inherits another person's adverse finding. Being taught
earns nothing at any age, and contributing earns recognition at any age, which
is the pair the 2026-08-02 doors ruling settled; recognition cannot pay, so it
cannot be the thing that makes a child's work worth taking.
