## Revision rulings D2 and D3 — 2026-09-24

The author ruled on the revision tracker's reserved questions on 2026-09-24;
`CLAUDE.md` records all nine under *The revision rulings D1–D9*. Two bear on
this decision. Items 53–60 implemented both, for the chapters, every Part
and the opening, and item 63 closed each derived account with a pointer to the
companion cases that run it.

**D2 — an argument section in every derived chapter.** Each derived chapter
keeps its pinned, flat account and ends with one labelled, first-person argument
section: the reason for the rule, the strongest alternative with its evidence,
and what would change the choice. Part V becomes a synthesis. For argument only,
this supersedes §5's closure of derived chapters to non-derived content and
§1a's refusal of labelled boxes inside derived chapters, together with the
voice-boundary limit of the author's first person to three elements. The
register of the derived sections stays flat, no record-person gains an inner
life, and composite citizens, invented antagonists and dramatised cast scenes
remain refused. The ordinary-life passage keeps its location rule. An argument
section argues for rules its chapter's derived sections state: no claim about
what the design does first appears there, every empirical claim carries a
registry source, and no argument creates a right, power or exception.

**D3 — documented cases outside Part V.** Documented, registry-sourced cases may
appear in the opening (Santoshi Kumari first, with the dispute over her death
intact), in a short labelled case opening each Part, and in argument sections.
This supersedes the rule that the historical cases stay in Part V. They never
appear in a derived section, they remain evidence about other societies rather
than a substitute for derived ordinary life, and no case gains an invented inner
life.

## 1. Decision

The narrative register **stays flat**. Record-people keep no inner life, the

What the decision refuses to do alongside that is the substance of it: it does

cannot show ordinary provision, care, or association today, and that is a
constitutional deficit rather than a prose deficit. Section 3 measures it,
section 8 classifies it, and section 9 gives it owners in the order they can

## 4. The rule for an ordinary-life passage

### 4a. Form

- Generic second person. **Never a cast name**, and never a name at all.
- **No interior state attributed to any person in the scene** — nobody feels,
  fears, hopes, or decides. A force named in the negative is permitted, because
  naming what cannot reach the household is a statement about the design rather
  than about a person's mind.
- No dialogue, no backstory, no named place, and no time-course beyond one
  scene — optionally paired with a contrast scene set in the reader's present
  society.
- It closes by re-declaring itself as argument rather than scene.

### 4b. Assertion

- **Every concrete detail traces**, and the passage's own sentence makes clear
  to which of three targets: a rule or fact in the constitution; a derived
  chapter's fixed gloss of a floor item; or a pinned fixture. A detail that
  traces to none of the three does not belong in the passage.
- **An arrival may appear only inside the hypothetical frame, and only where the
  same sentence names the entitlement that would ground it.** The frame is what
  makes the arrival claim-free; without the entitlement clause the sentence
  reads as a delivery promise the design does not keep.
- **Restatement only.** A passage may restate claims that already carry a
  posture elsewhere in Book 1. **No claim may make its first appearance in an
  exempt-element passage**, and a passage may never be the sole support for a
  ledger row, a coverage field, or any public claim.
- **The disclosure travels with it.** The passage carries, or sits near, the
  statement that the design delivers nothing outside confinement.

### 4c. The mood is claim-free; the predications are not

"Imagine a household" asserts nothing, so the frame introduces no claim and adds
no ledger row. That does **not** make every sentence inside the frame
claim-free: a definitional clause about the designed society, a description of
the reader's present world, and a universal negative about what cannot reach a
person are all predications, and they carry the postures they carry elsewhere.
The restatement rule in 4b is what keeps that safe — it forbids a claim from
living only in the one place nothing checks it.

A contrast scene about the reader's present society is permitted as rhetorical
contrast carrying no evidentiary weight. It is explicitly outside the sourcing
discipline that governs the rest of Part V, and it may not be cited as evidence
about any existing state.

### 4d. Mechanics

The slot sits inside the jargon sweep and the counted-claims hard gate, both of
which cover the numbered files. State the rule that produces any count, never
the count, and expect no new allowlist entry.

Derived chapters stay closed to non-derived content by three standing rulings.
No fourth exempt element is created.
