# Book 1 Family, Dependency, Reproduction, and Collective/Plurality Decision

> **Status: author-ratified on 2026-08-08; formal implementation pending.**
> This decision selects Book 1's family, dependency, reproduction,
> life-course, and collective/plurality baselines. It adds no predicate, rule,
> fact, pin, institution in law, chapter, release, age number, programme, or
> claim of present constitutional coverage.

## 1. Decision

Book 1 adopts **independent rights throughout the life course, modular family
recognition, public-first care, reproductive bodily authority, and
rights-bounded collective self-government**.

Every born child is a person with independent constitutional standing. General
legal adulthood is one automatic status acquired at the uniform age set by
common-tier law; it is not a maturity score or an official's permission.
Before that status, a child can exercise particular rights and decisions through
supported, decision-specific authority. After it, age, disability, diagnosis,
dependency, custody, or residence setting cannot remove legal agency by status
alone.

Family law must describe the legal role that produces an effect. Parentage,
caregiving, household membership, support, property, and decision agency are
separate relations. Pair and plural families may combine them without a
marriage label receiving constitutional priority. Children and dependants keep
rights, floors, voices, confidential help, and remedies of their own.

The public duty to preserve each person's floor and constitutional access is
immediate and non-delegable. Parents and people who expressly assume
responsible-care roles may owe bounded private duties, but affection, marriage,
kinship, co-residence, or dependency never becomes proof that the duty was
performed. Care remains valuable without becoming compulsory, scored, or
presumed.

Reproductive choice belongs to the pregnant person throughout pregnancy. The
constitution gives no embryo or fetus a separate constitutional personhood
capable of overriding that authority. Collective rights likewise add no
superior human status: Indigenous peoples and other protected communities
receive the capacities named here, while every member and nonmember retains the
universal individual floor, liberties, equality, due process, voice, exit, and
remedy.

This ruling works inside the already ratified democratic corridor,
substantive-equality baseline, protected voluntary sphere, federal state form,
residence-first political membership, economic settlement, and T3 boundary. It
does not constitutionalise one preferred household, belief, culture,
relationship, reproductive outcome, care arrangement, or personal identity.

## 2. Childhood, standing, and best interests

A born child holds the same universal standing as every other person. Childhood
changes how authority and support are exercised; it does not reduce the holder
to a parent's possession or evidence source. A child independently holds:

- every applicable material floor and equality protection;
- bodily integrity, privacy, conscience, family life, and protection from
  violence, exploitation, forced labour, and degrading treatment;
- accessible information about a decision that affects the child;
- a confidential route to advice, help, complaint, representation, review, and
  remedy;
- a right to express views through speech, sign, communication aid, behaviour,
  a chosen supporter, or another accessible mode; and
- continuity of education, health, care, identity records, significant
  relationships, and safe living conditions during a dispute.

No minimum speaking age controls the right to be heard. The decision-maker must
seek and record the child's views, provide communication and decision support,
give those views due weight in light of the particular decision and the child's
supported capacity, and explain the result accessibly. Silence, disability,
distress, institutional placement, or inability to use the official medium is
not consent and does not erase the duty to seek the child's perspective.

The child's best interests are a primary substantive right, interpretive
principle, and procedural consideration. They are not a label that makes an
adult's preference conclusive. A decision relying on best interests must state
the affected rights, the child's own views, the evidence, the available less
restrictive alternatives, the expected benefits and harms, the effect on
identity and significant relationships, and the route to independent review.
Where several children are affected, each receives an individual assessment;
one child's interests are not absorbed into a household average.

A parent or caregiver exercises fiduciary responsibility for the child's
protection and supported development. That authority is limited to the legal
function involved, diminishes as the child can act directly, and remains
challengeable. It cannot waive the child's standing, floor, bodily integrity,
privacy, equality, voice, or remedy.

## 3. General adulthood and early authority

The republic has one general legal-adulthood status. Common-tier law sets one
uniform age for it. This decision does not set the number.

Acquisition is automatic when the lawful age threshold is reached. A birth
record or other approved record is evidence of that event, not its source.
There is no application, official discretion, political maturity test, higher
candidacy age, property or contribution condition, medical certification, or
character assessment.

The evidence route must accept accessible alternatives where conventional birth
records are absent, incomplete, inaccessible, disputed, or wrong. It requires
notice, reasons, challenge, correction, an independent decision, and continuity.
A previously established adult remains an adult during a record dispute.
Raising the statutory age cannot return an adult to childhood or undo lawful
adult acts. A correction may govern future age-dependent events but cannot
retroactively erase rights validly exercised under the last uncontested record.

Where a person has no prior uncontested status and exact age cannot immediately
be established, missing documents cannot create indefinite childhood. A prompt,
independent, reviewable determination must use the best available evidence. If
credible evidence shows that the threshold may already have been crossed and
the state cannot resolve the record before a constitutional opportunity
expires, provisional adult status preserves that opportunity, including the
equal ballot and candidacy. Only a timely contrary determination supported by
evidence may withhold an age-specific power; uncertainty, administrative
silence, or documentation failure cannot do so indefinitely.

Before general adulthood, law may recognise supported authority for a particular
decision. The assessment must concern the information, consequences, support,
time, and communication involved in that decision; it cannot become a reusable
global capacity score. A lower-risk or rights-protective decision cannot be
blocked merely because a different, more complex decision would require more
support. A high-consequence conflict receives independent review, not an
automatic parent or institutional veto. Decision-specific early authority
cannot include the general-government ballot or candidacy; those arise only
from the general legal-adulthood status.

The exact supported early-authority tests remain a research and later
formalisation task. They must be evidence-based, accessible across communication
modes, resistant to adult convenience and coercion, and compatible with
confidential health and reproductive access. They cannot replace the automatic
general-adulthood status or create a second political threshold.

## 4. Modular family recognition

The constitution protects voluntary family and intimate life without selecting
one mandatory form. Pair and plural families, households, co-parenting
arrangements, caregiving networks, and people living alone receive equal
constitutional respect. Equal access cannot depend on sex, gender, sexual
orientation, fertility, disability, caste, religion, nationality, property,
custody, or conformity with a conventional household.

Law must identify the direct legal effect instead of asking only whether someone
is “family.” At minimum it separates:

- **parentage:** the enduring legal relation to a child;
- **caregiving:** a present responsibility to provide or coordinate specified
  care;
- **household:** a chosen or factual living arrangement;
- **support:** a defined financial or material obligation;
- **property:** ownership, beneficial interest, use, and winding-up claims; and
- **decision agency:** authority to support or represent another person for a
  specified decision.

An adult may combine one or more of these modules with one or more other adults.
Each adult entering a legal module must give free and informed consent to its
defined duties and consequences. Material shared obligations require disclosure
to the adults whose existing legal claims would be altered; disclosure does not
create a veto over another person's harmless intimacy or association.

Every arrangement requires intelligible entry, amendment, exit, dissolution,
property, support, record, and dispute rules. Exit must be realistically
available and cannot remove the exiting person's floor, remedy, or claim for
violence, labour, care, property, or support already owed. A private agreement
cannot waive a child's rights, bind a person who did not consent, or contract
out of equality, bodily integrity, labour protection, public continuity, or due
process.

Marriage may remain a private, cultural, religious, or statutory label. The
label alone receives no constitutional priority in parentage, support,
property, immigration, public benefit, succession, decision agency, or remedy.
Law may attach effects only by identifying and justifying the corresponding
module. No religious or cultural body controls access to the republic's civil
family relations.

## 5. Parentage, origin, and child continuity

Parentage is responsibility and relationship, never ownership. Law may establish
it through birth, prior reproductive intent, voluntary acknowledgment, adoption,
or an adjudicated child-continuity finding. Biology, gestation, marriage,
payment, household membership, or registration alone is neither universally
necessary nor universally sufficient.

More than two legal parents may be recognised where prior intent, adoption, or
an individualized continuity finding establishes that relation consistently
with the child's rights and best interests. The addition of a parent cannot
dilute every responsible person's duties into nothing, cancel an existing
parent without process, or leave the child unable to identify a responsible
person. Responsibilities must be allocated expressly, and public continuity
remains available if any or all responsible people fail.

Adoption and parentage proceedings require the child's age-appropriate
information and voice, the free and informed consent of every adult whose
consent lawfully controls, independent review, and protection against sale,
coercion, deception, or status gaps. A child's poverty, disability, ancestry,
or a parent's custody status cannot be converted into consent or abandonment.

Accurate birth, adoption, donor, gestational-origin, and parentage records must
be preserved under purpose-limited privacy and integrity controls. The person
whose origin is recorded has an accessible route to identity and material
health information. Access may protect another person's current contact details
or unrelated private information, but it cannot replace the origin record with
a deliberate falsehood. Learning an identity creates no automatic right to
contact, custody, support, inheritance, or a personal relationship.

Imprisonment, institutional placement, disability, migration status, or
household form never automatically ends parentage, contact, custody, or the
child's relationship rights. A restriction requires an individualized lawful
basis, necessity, proportionality, the child's voice, review, continuity, and
remedy.

## 6. Care, dependency, and private duties

The public duty to secure the material floor, accessible services, and urgent
continuity belongs to public institutions. It cannot be transferred to a
parent, partner, household, charity, collective, or unpaid caregiver. Evidence
that voluntary private care currently meets a need may prevent unwanted
duplicate action; it does not extinguish the public duty or entitlement.

Parents and people who expressly undertake a responsible-care role owe duties
within that role and their actual capacity. Those duties include reasonable
care, protection, coordination, information, and prompt use of the public
continuity route where private care cannot be supplied. A person cannot avoid
an assumed duty by relabelling the relationship, but neither kinship nor
co-residence alone silently creates it.

Every dependant keeps an independent entitlement, confidential help route,
advocate, privacy, property claim, voice, and exit. Every caregiver keeps an
independent floor, bodily integrity, privacy, rest, respite, income protection
under separately enacted law, freedom from violence, and a route to relinquish
or change an unsustainable role safely. One person's dependency cannot erase
another person's rights; one person's exhaustion cannot erase the dependant's
continuity.

Adult kinship creates no compulsory personal-care duty and no inherited support
debt. Law may enforce a clearly assumed support module, an adjudicated
responsibility for harm or induced dependency, or an estate obligation, but it
may not conscript affection or bodily service. General financing of care belongs
through public revenue and social insurance, not a kinship debt imposed on a
particular adult child or relative.

Care may be paid work, unpaid voluntary care, reciprocal support, or a mixed
arrangement. Employment and labour rights attach where the actual control and
dependency test establishes work. Neither `work(Care)`, recognition, love,
family status, nor household membership proves that a person's needs were met
or that a caregiver consented to employment. Care periods may support portable
social insurance without converting affection into wages, merit, or `reward`.

## 7. Domestic protection, intervention, and separation

Private and family life is protected against prescription, surveillance, and
official outcome certification. Unconventional, plural, single-person,
intergenerational, cultural, religious, or communal living is not evidence of
harm. Poverty, disability, custody, migration status, identity, belief, or
family form alone cannot justify investigation, removal, guardianship, or
separation.

Public protection duties arise on credible evidence of violence, sexual abuse,
coercive control, forced labour, exploitation, serious neglect, confinement,
economic captivity, confiscation or control of documents, obstruction of
confidential help, or denial of free exit. A person may request information,
shelter, healthcare, advocacy, or continuity without first proving an offence
or agreeing to end a relationship.

Protective action must target the evidenced danger rather than dissolve a
household by default. It requires lawful authority, reasons, necessity,
proportionality, accessible notice where safe, the affected person's voice,
confidential support, independent challenge, and a route to restoration and
repair. It cannot compel reconciliation, mediation, disclosure, or contact with
the person alleged to have caused harm.

An immediate danger may support temporary emergency protection before an
ordinary hearing. That authority requires its own narrow trigger, least-
restrictive scope, alternate reviewer, source-bound T3 end, prompt review,
continuity, and correction. Silence by a reviewer is never continuing approval.

Separating a child from a parent or primary caregiver is a last resort. It
requires individualized evidence of serious harm or neglect, practical support
before removal where support can make the placement safe, attention to sibling
and significant relationships, and the least-disruptive safe alternative.
Every order must include independent representation, periodic review, and
either a supported reunification path or a reasoned stable-care plan.

## 8. Supported legal agency

Every adult enjoys legal capacity on an equal basis. Disability, diagnosis,
communication mode, age, institutional residence, dependency, unconventional
choice, or another person's view of welfare does not by itself prove
incapacity. Plenary guardianship and status-based removal of legal agency are
refused.

A person may choose, change, limit, or refuse one or more supporters. Support
must be accessible, proportionate to the requested decision, free of conflict
and undue influence, and guided by the person's will and preferences. A
supporter explains, communicates, assists, and records; support does not
silently become substituted choice. Refusal of support cannot remove the
person's floor or access to an independent route.

Where a person's current will and preferences remain unascertainable after
genuine support, law may use only a conflict-free, decision-specific,
least-restrictive, time-bounded best interpretation of the person's will and
preferences. It must use known values, past and present expressions, trusted
relationships chosen by the person, and an independently reviewable record.
General “best interests,” institutional convenience, diagnosis, or family
preference cannot substitute for that inquiry.

An emergency act taken to prevent imminent serious and otherwise unavoidable
harm must be no broader or longer than necessary and cannot establish continuing
incapacity. It triggers notice, independent review, correction, restoration,
and remedy. Each later decision begins again from the person's legal capacity;
an earlier support or emergency record is not a reusable personal score.

Advance directives, powers chosen by the person, support agreements, and
best-interpretation findings are consequential records. Each requires
authentication, scope, challenge, correction, conflict control, carry, end, and
revocation rules. No representative acquires general authority from one narrow
decision.

## 9. Reproductive autonomy and assisted reproduction

Every person has equal access to accurate reproductive information,
contraception, fertility assistance, pregnancy care, childbirth care,
postpartum care, sterilization decisions, abortion care, and remedy, within the
applicable health and material-floor contracts.

Reproductive decisions require the person's free and informed choice. Forced
or coerced contraception, sterilization, impregnation, pregnancy, abortion,
birth method, fertility treatment, or genetic testing is prohibited. A spouse,
partner, parent, supporter, guardian, institution, provider, donor, or state
official receives no authorization veto merely from that status.

The pregnant person controls whether to continue or terminate a pregnancy
throughout pregnancy. No embryo or fetus has separate constitutional
personhood, standing, or a claim that overrides the pregnant person's bodily
authority. Law may protect prenatal health only through measures compatible
with that authority, informed consent, equality, privacy, and non-punitive
care. Pregnancy outcomes cannot become evidence of guilt, unfitness, worth, or
loss of unrelated rights.

A child capable of a supported reproductive or health decision receives a
confidential decision route under section 3. A parent is not an automatic veto
or mandatory disclosure destination. Where a high-consequence capacity or
safety dispute remains, a prompt independent route must protect confidentiality
and timely care rather than allow delay to decide the issue.

An individual professional's conscientious refusal cannot control another
person's outcome. It may be accommodated only where there is no emergency and
an immediate, effective, accessible transfer preserves timely care. An
institution, monopoly provider, or public system remains responsible for
continuity.

Assisted reproduction and gestational agreements are lawful subject to free
and informed consent, independent advice, equality, privacy, anti-exploitation,
record integrity, and child-continuity safeguards. An agreement cannot control
a pregnant person's body, medical choices, movement, or decision to continue or
terminate a pregnancy. No breach term may punish the exercise of those rights.

Parentage must be established without leaving the child in a legal-status gap.
Democratic law may regulate reimbursement, compensation, brokerage, and
commercial participation, but payment cannot purchase a child, bodily control,
silence, or false origin records. Donors, intended parents, gestational parents,
providers, and intermediaries remain subject to the equality, contract,
consumer, labour, privacy, and private-power settlements applicable to their
actual functions.

## 10. Ageing, treatment, and end of life

Ageing creates no automatic loss of home, community, legal agency, property,
privacy, political membership, or bodily authority. Public institutions must
support accessible community living, care, communication, pain relief, and
palliative care. Institutional placement requires consent or the same narrow,
reviewable authority that would justify it for any other adult; age or
caregiver convenience is insufficient.

A person's current informed decision controls treatment, including consent,
refusal, withholding, and withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment. If no current
decision can be ascertained after support, authority proceeds in this order:

1. a valid and applicable advance directive;
2. a representative previously chosen by the person, applying known will and
   preferences; then
3. the bounded best-interpretation route in section 8.

Family rank, marriage, payment, inheritance expectations, provider convenience,
or a generalized view of quality of life cannot displace that order. A dispute
requires an expedited independent review and continued comfort, communication,
and non-disputed care.

Medically assisted dying is democratically permissible for adults; it is
neither a constitutional entitlement nor constitutionally prohibited. Any
enabling law must require the person's explicit, informed, contemporaneous, and
repeated request; independent capacity and voluntariness review; protection
against pressure, abuse, and conflicts; access to real care, disability support,
income, housing, and palliative alternatives; a withdrawal route through the
last possible point; and an independent audit and remedy. A representative,
advance directive, family member, or best-interpretation process cannot supply
the request.

Any assisted-dying authority requires its own source-bound temporal contract.
Custody T3 cannot be reused, and Book 1 cannot claim that an outside clock,
waiting period, clinician, reviewer, or service operates merely because a
recorded condition is derivable.

Death and its medical determination are authenticated external findings, not
Nibli conclusions. A person's valid directions concerning remains, privacy, and
culturally compatible disposition receive effect within public-health,
equality, criminal-investigation, and collective-title limits. No survivor
acquires ownership of the deceased person's identity or confidential history
merely through family rank.

## 11. Succession

A valid will leads the distribution of an estate, subject to lawful estate
liabilities, bounded dependant-protection claims, collective-title
restrictions, taxation, equality, and anti-domination rules. Public office,
political weight, human standing, guilt, punishment, and debt beyond the estate
cannot be inherited.

Intestacy must follow functional legal relationships rather than marital rank.
Democratic law may specify shares and priority among children, parents,
expressly assumed partners or support relations, and other evidenced
interdependence, but it cannot make a marriage label the sole route to
recognition or erase a child because of birth, adoption, donor conception,
gestation, or plural parentage.

An individual will cannot transfer or extinguish collective land or resource
title contrary to the collective's lawful tenure rules. It may transfer only
the interest the person actually held. A succession dispute cannot interrupt
the floor, evict a dependant without the housing-continuity process, conceal
assets or origin records, or permit a prospective beneficiary to control care
or end-of-life decisions.

This ruling sets no forced-share percentage, tax rate, probate hierarchy,
estate-valuation rule, or operational succession process.

## 12. Collective and plurality rights

The republic protects both individual plurality and specified collective
rights. A collective holds only the constitutional capacities assigned to it;
it does not receive human standing, a material floor, a personal ballot,
candidacy, or inherent superiority over its members or neighbours.

Indigenous peoples receive protected rights to:

- maintain and develop their identity, institutions, language, culture,
  spirituality, knowledge, education, media, and relationships across
  generations;
- govern internal and local affairs through institutions of their choosing;
- participate through their own representatives in public decisions affecting
  them;
- hold, use, govern, protect, and transmit collective lands, waters, resources,
  and culturally significant places under constitutionally protected title;
- receive restitution, restoration, compensation, or another effective remedy
  for continuing unlawful dispossession, destruction, or forced assimilation;
  and
- maintain cross-boundary cultural and kin relations consistently with the
  separately ratified external and security rules when those rules exist.

Linguistic, religious, ethnic, national, cultural, and other minorities receive
rights to maintain and develop language, culture, belief, education, media,
association, institutions, and community life; to receive accessible public
information and services; to participate effectively in decisions affecting
them; and to resist forced assimilation, imposed segregation, and cultural
destruction.

Internal cultural or religious practice remains voluntary. A collective cannot
use autonomy to impose belief, block exit or confidential help, assign guilt by
membership, confiscate documents or property, force labour or marriage, silence
internal dissent, deny a child education or healthcare, or reduce any person's
standing, equality, floor, bodily integrity, due process, political equality,
or remedy.

Territorial authority requires an independently established historical or
territorial basis. Indigenous self-government and collective title receive the
specific protection stated here. Another territorially concentrated people may
receive regional or local self-government through the federal, subsidiarity,
equality, and democratic routes; cultural difference alone does not create an
unbounded territorial jurisdiction.

## 13. Membership, internal government, and individual rights

Collective membership requires the person's self-identification and the
collective's lawful acceptance process. Neither side alone can fabricate the
relation. The process must be public enough to be knowable, consistent with the
collective's continuity, free from prohibited discrimination, and open to an
independent procedural challenge without requiring public disclosure of
sensitive identity information.

Multiple collective memberships are permitted. No state body may demand one
exclusive identity where the memberships can coexist. A person may leave an
internal governing or associative relation without losing universal rights,
common services, a general-government ballot, or a remedy for prior conduct.
Exit does not falsify historical origin or automatically extinguish a lawful
property or land interest; those consequences require their own title rules.

Internal selection, representation, and customary law may differ from the
republic's general-government electoral mechanics. They remain subject to
universal standing, substantive equality, bodily integrity, core liberty,
children's rights, due process, individual voice, access to reasons, and
effective appeal. An affected person may reach an independent Constitutional
Court for final constitutional review. Review protects the corridor; it does
not make that Court the ordinary administrator of internal life.

A collective representative must derive authority from the collective's
lawful process, disclose conflicts, act within scope, and remain challengeable.
The state cannot manufacture consent by selecting a convenient spokesperson,
and a self-declared representative cannot bind the collective by assertion
alone. Silence, missing participation, or unavailable records never becomes
consent.

Residents who are not members retain equal general-government voting,
protection, common services, property and housing rights, due process, and
remedy within an autonomous territory. Collective institutions may govern
genuinely internal membership, culture, and collective-title matters; they
cannot turn neighbouring residents into subjects without the ordinary
residence-first democratic route.

## 14. Collective title, consent, and consultation

Free, prior, informed, and actual collective consent is required before public
or delegated power may authorize:

- permanent relocation from collective lands;
- extinguishment or irreversible impairment of collective land or resource
  title;
- destruction of a sacred or uniquely irreplaceable cultural place;
- storage or disposal of hazardous material on collective lands; or
- another measure with a comparably existential and irreversible effect on the
  collective's continued life.

Collective consent is necessary but does not by itself authorize violence,
detention, or forced movement of a particular person. Individual bodily,
housing, child, disability, equality, and due-process rights continue to apply.
Any agreement requires accessible information, adequate time, independent
advice, an authenticated representative process, conflict disclosure, a public
record of scope, challenge, and a remedy for deception, coercion, or material
breach.

A temporary evacuation to prevent imminent loss of life may occur without prior
collective consent only under a separate emergency contract. It must be
necessary, least restrictive, source-bound, independently reviewed, supported
by safe continuity, and followed by return and repair. It cannot transfer or
extinguish title. The emergency ruling of 2026-08-08 supplies that contract and
does not weaken this gate: it creates no derogation power, so an evacuation
suspends no right and no title, and it must satisfy the declaration's named
hazard, separate justification, published reasons, cross-branch authorisation,
per-renewal temporal contract, judicial review, restoration, and post-hoc audit.
That ruling separately makes actual collective consent a gate on any negotiated
exit that would transfer, extinguish, or irreversibly impair collective title.
See
[`public-safety-defence-emergency-and-external-power-decision.md`](public-safety-defence-emergency-and-external-power-decision.md).

For other laws, projects, licences, or public decisions with a material
collective effect, the duty is good-faith consultation and accommodation:
timely accessible information, participation through lawful representatives
and affected members, adequate time, consideration of alternatives, a reasoned
response, mitigation, benefit and burden review, challenge, and correction.
This is not a blanket veto, and consultation is not satisfied by notice after
the decision is fixed.

Collective land and resource governance remains bounded by the universal floor,
the rights of members and lawful nonmember residents, interregional obligations,
the separately ratified scarcity contract, and the later ecological, commons,
and direct animal-protection baselines. Collective consent is necessary for the
existential harms listed above, but it cannot authorize breach of a binding
ecological ceiling or waive direct protection owed to a sentient non-human
animal.

Conversely, ecological or animal-protection law cannot be used as a pretext for
dispossession, assimilation, forced cultural abandonment, or exclusion from
evidence and remedy. Every human-controlled animal use remains subject to the
non-waivable welfare baseline. An Indigenous, cultural, or religious lethal,
invasive, or high-severity use receives neither a blanket exemption nor automatic
prohibition: its purpose, necessity, alternatives, and least-harm evidence must
be assessed under the separate animal-protection contract with equality,
consultation, and independent review.

## 15. Public authority, federal allocation, and remedies

The common tier owns the constitutional minimum: universal individual rights,
the general-adulthood framework, protection against forced assimilation,
recognition of the collective capacities named here, cross-regional
portability, and final constitutional review. It must respect the protected
regional, local, and collective competences already allocated by the federal
settlement.

Regional, local, and collective institutions may enact stronger protections
and administer lawful family, care, language, culture, land, and local-
government arrangements. They may not lower common standing, floors, equality,
labour rights, bodily integrity, due process, political equality, or remedy.
Competence conflicts use reasons, mediation, continuity, and Constitutional
Court review; delay gives neither side new permanent authority.

Each domain requires independently assigned initiation, investigation,
adjudication, execution, audit, and final-review functions. Affected children
and adults, chosen supporters, qualified associations, lawful collective
representatives, and independent child, disability, equality, rights, or ombud
functions may initiate within their proper scope. Representation cannot erase
the affected person's voice or waive that person's rights.

Available relief must include, as applicable:

- immediate floor and service continuity;
- cessation and protection from retaliation;
- accessible decision support, independent representation, and an alternate
  route around a conflicted or unavailable decision-maker;
- correction of adulthood, parentage, origin, support, membership, title, and
  authority records while preserving provenance;
- restoration of contact, agency, property, housing, work, support, land,
  cultural access, or collective authority;
- reunification or a stable-care order after a child-separation case;
- restitution, compensation or reparation, rehabilitation, and non-repetition;
  and
- a bounded structural order where repeated failure shows a shared defect.

A remedy cannot remove another person's standing, floor, bodily integrity,
core liberty, due process, equal general-government ballot, or effective
remedy. Collective repair cannot assign guilt solely by group membership or
erase the voice of an affected individual. Individual relief cannot extinguish
a valid collective title.

General-government elections retain one equal ballot for every adult ordinary
resident. Collective internal selection is a distinct constitutional function,
not a second general-government electorate. Collective autonomy creates no
unilateral secession right; the existing negotiated, rights-reviewed federal
route remains controlling.

## 16. Book 1, Book 2, and engine boundary

Book 1 owns the constitutional holders, sources, duties, liberties, limits,
evidence contracts, burden and review rules, failure polarity, continuity,
remedies, federal allocation, and structural refusals selected here. It must
describe normal, failure, challenge, correction, restoration, and recovery
interfaces without pretending to operate them.

Land, title, culture, and consultation operations must interoperate with Book
2's separate ecological, commons, and animal-protection handoff. Neither handoff
may redefine the other's legal holders, consent boundary, ceiling, direct animal
protection, or remedy.

Book 2 owns childcare, education, family proceeding, adoption, reproductive,
fertility, birth, abortion, disability-support, home and community support,
caregiver-respite, palliative, assisted-dying, probate, land/title, language,
consultation, and restitution operations. That includes staffing, training,
facilities, caseloads, capacity, costs, supply, access routes, records
technology, privacy operations, clocks, publication, inspection, referral,
cross-border coordination, transition, deployment, and empirical feasibility.
Book 2 remains inactive until Book 1 ships at Gate C.

The operational handoff must preserve separate flows for children, parents,
caregivers, dependants, supporters, pregnant people, patients, donors,
gestational participants, collectives, representatives, members, nonmembers,
rights advocates, reviewers, providers, and public bodies. It must test access,
capacity, delay, coercion, capture, conflict, continuity, correction,
portability, cost, and failure without redefining a legal right.

Nibli may consume an authenticated, bounded finding such as established
adulthood, parentage, chosen support, a valid directive, a current consent,
death, collective membership, representative authority, title, consultation,
or certified collective consent. It must not be credited with:

- deciding best interests, maturity, decision capacity, consent, parentage,
  pregnancy, medical facts, death, dependency, household safety, collective
  identity, membership, territorial basis, title, or cultural significance;
- authenticating a person, record, representative, clinician, reviewer,
  collective process, clock, publication, or institutional act;
- computing a dynamic vote threshold or converting a tally into legal effect;
  or
- delivering care, performing a procedure, moving a person, transferring
  property, enforcing an order, or operating a remedy.

Apply the completed finite collective-decision audit to any family,
institutional, or collective vote. A supplied result certificate remains an
external trust-root premise. The rule card must state the roster,
classification, completeness, threshold, conflict, tie, empty-roster,
alternate-attestation, challenge, correction, and failure defaults. A fixed

affirmatively preserve a current arrangement.

Every temporary custody, protection, placement, support, reproductive,
treatment, assisted-dying, title, consultation, or emergency authority requires

## 17. Formal migration contract

This ruling is **ratified, unimplemented**. Formal work begins with a fresh
producer/consumer census and one rule-family card per direct legal effect.
There is no omnibus `family`, `collective`, `care`, `capacity`, or
`reproduction` predicate.

Before adding or rewiring vocabulary:

1. replace the present asserted `mature` gate with the automatic general-
   adulthood contract and separate supported, decision-specific early
   authority; do not silently widen `mature`;
2. retire unary `family` as a placement, risk, liberty, delivery, or
   responsibility proxy and replace each legal effect with a typed,
   case-specific relation;
3. split `home` into secure-dwelling delivery, residence, political home,
   possession/use, household, placement, and collective-title concepts where
   their direct effects differ;
4. replace absence-based `parent`, `married`, and `sibling`
   independence tests with affirmative, case-bound conflict and independence
   findings; missing kinship evidence never certifies independence;
5. never reuse `work(Care)`, `healthy`, `free`, `public`, `owe`,
   recognition, custody, conviction, or household status as proof of care,
   consent, emancipation, adulthood, parentage, collective status, title, or
   responsibility; and
6. preserve `admits`, `derived_only`, the floor anti-sanction firewall,
   recognition's non-operation and arity, the no-score guards, equality's data
   wall, and the current temporal assurance as distinct controls.

Each consequential status or authority card must name its holder, duty-bearer,
source, authorised writer, admissible evidence, independent reader, scope,
privacy boundary, conflict rule, challenge, correction, carry, end condition,
alternate route, interim continuity, remedy, audit, and failure polarity.
Registration and a derived status are evidence and legal effect inside the
model; they are never proof that the outside event or institution exists.

Classify each rule by direct legal effect under the ratified constitutional
taxonomy. A mixed proposal becomes separate cards for standing, liberty,
public duty, institution, record, commons, or other existing class. Collective
rights are specified legal capacities and restraints, not a new human
personhood class.

Receipt and delivery evidence for care, health, reproductive services,
language access, support, or community living must be medium-neutral and
recipient-side. It cannot assert a desired personal state, presume household
delivery, or use `teaches` plus `hears` as a general template.

Before a formal family lands, audit at minimum Chapters 1, 3, 5, 6, 8–11,
13–14, every additional chapter found by the producer/consumer census, Part
V's rotation, State, care, and narrowness-dependent verdicts, `method.md`,
the placement and temporal assurance cases, every affected TRUE and FALSE pin,
registries, counterfactuals, generated assertion surfaces, and counted-claim
guards.

The impact review must explicitly revisit:

- family status worsening confinement and absence of family affecting
  placement;
- marriage-centric and absence-based independence claims;
- `mature` as a writer-controlled political gate;
- child work and teaching being treated as safe merely because recognition
  does not pay;
- recognition-as-care and recognition-as-compensation metaphors;
- `home` being mistaken for ownership, residence, household, or delivered

- Chapter 13's single-deprivation claim and title;
- Chapter 14's inert-obligation ending after remedy readers exist; and
- Part V or method claims that stay textually unchanged while another rule

A claim must be revised whenever its truth changes, even if no local pin or
derivation in that chapter moves. A retired claim's prose, registry entry,
counterfactual, generated report, and counted-claim guard change atomically.

Ratification does not satisfy Gate B or Gate C. Coverage becomes formal only
after the rule families, pins, negative controls, counterfactuals, reviewed
audits, generated spine, derived prose, and reader contract agree.
