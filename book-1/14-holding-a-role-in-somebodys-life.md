# Holding a Role in Somebody's Life

Helping somebody leaves their decisions with them. This chapter separates care
from authority, a chosen supporter from a substitute decision, and a person's
own voice from the record made about it.

The case that tests it is a person nobody is recorded as acting for. A
first-contact entry makes them a person, and a reviewed appointment names a
representative for one matter and a receipt witness. The appointment
concludes nothing adverse about them and gives the representative no parentage
authority. Help has a named bearer, and the person's need stays a need rather
than becoming a status of incapacity.

## What kinship does not create

**Care is not a family invoice.** Public responsibility for care continuity is
immediate and non-delegable: naming a parent, partner, child or other relative
leaves it where it is. A caregiver and a dependant each keep a separate floor,
privacy, voice, exit and remedy. Kinship alone places no compulsory
personal-care duty or inherited support debt on an adult. A record of care as
employment identifies an activity and nothing more: whether the care was safe,
whether anyone consented, and whether the worker gained any authority over the
person cared for each need their own evidence.

## Each role has its own limits

Parentage, caregiving, household membership, material support, property and
succession, and decision agency have distinct records, and each confers only
itself. Authority that comes with a role is a challengeable fiduciary power for
a named decision, never ownership of a person, and it comes from the role's own
record alone, whatever marriage or household the people share. Each caregiver
and dependant keeps separate standing, voice, privacy, confidential help and
exit.

## The person must be heard

The person must be heard without a minimum speaking age. The participation
record holds their own account without certifying a preference or relationship.
Where the adults' interests conflict, independent assistance is required.
Authority grows decision by decision rather than arriving as a score for a whole
person. The participation record must match the exact role it concerns, so a
role reviewed for one decision domain licenses nothing in another. Whoever acts
for a person in several domains therefore needs a reviewed role in each.

## Support assists; it does not replace

A supporter is chosen, can be changed and can be refused. While support can
still be given, a substituted decision is barred. Plenary guardianship and
status-based incapacity are refused, and so is any global capability score.

Where will and preferences genuinely remain unascertainable after support was
actually provided, and the record holds positive evidence that it was, a
bounded route opens. It is conflict-free, decision-specific and
least-restrictive; it is time-bounded and independently reviewed before and
during; and it ends when preferences become ascertainable. Take the
support-first evidence out of the record and the route stays closed. The last
resort is reached only through the step before it.

The order costs time. A decision that support could make possible waits for the
support, and the person, with anyone relying on the decision, bears the wait.

## Care continuity needs nobody to hold a role

Public responsibility for care continuity is immediate and non-delegable. Its
reviewed record can complete with every family role absent: no family,
household, association or charity is presumed to supply the floor. The duty is
owed with or without a chosen supporter. Respite, exit and relocation must
preserve care rather than making a relationship the price of receiving it.

The continuity conclusion follows only from its own recorded premises, and a
family's absence adds nothing to them. The ordinary case turns on independence
from a family role.

## Reproductive and bodily care, and the order of a treatment decision

Reproductive and bodily care needs no third party's authorisation.
Current informed choice controls, including refusal. These records open no
route from that choice to punishment or an adverse finding about the person.
For treatment more broadly the order is fixed: the current choice, then a valid
advance directive on its own terms, then a chosen representative implementing
known will and preferences, and only then the bounded route. Age, a diagnosis
and place of residence, by themselves, give nobody authority to override that
choice.

## Your own origin record

Birth, adoption, donor and gestational-origin records must be kept accurately,
and the person the record is about can reach and correct their own. That access
brings no automatic contact or relationship right in either direction, and a
correction leaves the lawful history intact.

## When one of these records is defective

An independent defect finding withdraws exactly what it names and creates
duties to correct and publish the correction. A request for review goes ahead
without the acting body's permission, and a certified failure to answer moves
the duty to an independent alternate.

## When nobody holds a role at all

The independent rights advocate owes every person a duty to initiate assistance
and representation without a request. A reviewed appointment specifies how
that duty is to be performed in a particular matter.

It must identify a recognised trigger: nobody is recorded as acting for the
person; the sole supporter is accused of failing them; accessible contact is
unavailable; the person is held or placed without representation; or an earlier
appointment ended without a successor. An invented trigger completes nothing.

The appointment completes only for someone a standing encounter has reached: a
birth, first contact, presence within the republic or under its power, or a
report that nobody is acting for them.

The appointment names a representative for that matter alone, as distinct from
a guardian over the person's life. It preserves their voice and stays free of
any capability finding, reusable classification or enrolment. Its information
is limited to what the matter needs. A source-bound end prevents silent
renewal, and the care continuity duty and the floor stay in force through any
gap in representation.

It also names a receipt witness. When the appointment completes, the acting
body owes a duty to authorise that witness, and the appointment itself leaves
the witness unauthorised. The representative owes action within the appointed
matter; the office that hears challenges owes a hearing and correction. The
appointment concludes nothing adverse about the participant, whose standing
comes from the encounter, and gives the representative no parentage authority.
Appointment, witness authority and authority over the person are separate
conclusions.

Separately authorised source, evidence and review attesters must agree on the
fields. The representative must be someone other than the acting body, the
reviewer, the office that hears challenges, the alternate and the affected
person. A conflicting authorised entry on a single-valued field blocks
completion. An independent alternate supports the challenge route. These
conditions complete a reviewed appointment record.

## Adulthood and missing evidence

Cira is a person and is owed the floor. The record lacks evidence in the
required form that Cira has reached the common adulthood threshold, so the
rules stop short of a ballot conclusion. That settles nothing about Cira's age.
To the record, *not yet* adult and adult with missing evidence look the same,
because the rules read no outside clock.

In the law, reaching the common age creates general adulthood automatically;
registration only evidences it. Early decision-specific authority can cover a
named choice without conferring general adulthood or a ballot. The executable
rules still need recorded age evidence before they conclude the ordinary
franchise.

Esa illustrates the gap. Teaching, having been examined and being somebody's
recorded child say nothing about age. Esa's teaching remains recorded, and
without an adulthood entry the rules stop short of a ballot conclusion. The
absence says nothing about whether Esa is a child.

Esa can challenge the missing or disputed age evidence. The challenge creates
a named dispute and a duty of independent determination and correction; the age
and the missing entry are left to that determination. The last uncontested
adult status also continues, and a separate provisional opportunity is
available where credible evidence indicates the common threshold may have been
crossed and a ballot or candidacy would otherwise expire. Each route needs
positive evidence; missing evidence and a reviewer's silence give no approval.

The distinction from disenfranchisement is legal as well as formal. Withholding
an already held franchise as punishment differs from a threshold not yet
reached. Missing evidence can nevertheless block an executable conclusion about
an entitlement already held in law, and the person whose evidence is missing
bears that gap. The challenge and the provisional route address it without
proving anyone's birthday. *The Vote Conviction Does Not Take* follows those
routes.

## The child with nobody

Nell's record holds one entry, a birth, and names nobody in any role. The
public care duties remain, and the rights advocate's duty to initiate
assistance and representation follows without a request. The State owes Nell
care and company while the record shows neither delivered. The barriers hold
for Nell as for everyone: kinship creates no compulsory personal-care debt, and
a family's absence authorises no confinement.

The appointment described above shows what the route requires and appoints
nobody for this child. A substitute decision for Nell would still need the
evidence that support came first. The record names a duty-bearer and nobody
holding representation or witness authority.

## What this cannot settle

The bounded route opens on two recorded findings: that support was provided,
and that the person's will and preferences remained unascertainable after it.
The rules check that separately authorised people recorded both and that the
route is reviewed independently. Whether the support was real, and whether
anyone could have understood the person, is decided by the people who make
those records.

Best interests, capacity, consent, pregnancy, parentage, age and death are
likewise determined outside the rules and reach them only as entries. Whether a
child was heard, a supporter arrived or care continued, and the service
capacity, clinical practice and family proceedings behind those facts, belong
to the second book.

Through every role the boundary is the same: assistance confers no ownership,
and the absence of a private helper leaves public responsibility in place. The
next chapter takes that boundary across a border.

## Argument: Support before substitution

*The companion states these rules as [Article 13](https://dhilipsiva.dev/rights-nobody-has-to-earn/constitution/#article-13).*

I choose to treat each role in a person's life as a separable power, held for
them over a named decision and open to challenge, because a role that travels
across decisions becomes a status over the person, the status plenary
guardianship confers. Support comes first for the same reason, and a
best-interpretation route opens only when support has been given and the
person's will still cannot be read. Article 12 of the Convention on the Rights
of Persons with Disabilities recognises that disabled people enjoy legal
capacity on an equal basis with others, requires access to the support they may
need, and asks that measures respect the person's will and preferences and be
conflict-free, proportional, as brief as possible and regularly
reviewed;[^crpd] the bounded route's conditions answer to those safeguards.
Its committee reads the article as requiring supported decision-making in place
of substitute regimes, and where will and preferences cannot be determined
after significant efforts, the best interpretation of them replaces a
determination of best interests.[^crpd-gc1] That is the committee's reading,
stated for adults, and I adopt it.

The child is heard at any age for a parallel reason. Article 12 of the
Convention on the Rights of the Child gives a child capable of forming views the
right to express them freely in all matters affecting the child, weighed by age
and maturity; its committee reads it as imposing no age limit and asks states
to presume that a child can form views.[^crc] Recording the account without
certifying a preference lets the youngest be heard without their words becoming
a choice.

The strongest alternative is best-interests substitute decision-making, as the
Mental Capacity Act 2005 enacts it for England and Wales. Its best case is how
much support it already demands: it presumes capacity, forbids treating anyone
as unable to decide until all practicable steps to help have failed, and judges
capacity one matter at a time. After a finding of incapacity for a decision,
another person decides in the person's best interests, weighing their wishes,
feelings, beliefs and values, encouraging participation and consulting carers;
a decision-maker other than a court complies, having followed those steps, by
reasonably believing the act serves those interests.[^mca] The wishes count
without deciding, and somebody can always decide.

Against it, my rule costs time and decisiveness. A decision waits while support
can still be given, and the person and anyone relying on it bear the wait.
Where the Act accepts reasonable belief, the bounded route asks for recorded
support, independent review and an end, and carers and families bear that
procedure. A best interpretation of someone's will may choose what a
best-interests decision-maker would judge worse for them, and the person bears
that risk, as anyone deciding for themselves bears the risk of an unwise choice.

I would reconsider if people whose will could not be read after support came to
more harm under a best-interpretation route than comparable people under
best-interests decisions, or if support-first left needed decisions waiting;
no study comparing the two in practice is cited here, so the choice rests on
how each is built. For children, I would
reconsider if recording a young child's account led offices to treat it as the
child's own decision.
