## manifesto.md

Its structure is inherited, not designed: sixteen chapters mapped one-to-one onto the sixteen lines of Bharathiar's *யோக சித்தி* (the வரம் கேட்டல் stanzas — the same passage `book.md`'s appendix attributes and sources to Project Madurai), eight of lament and eight of petition. That's why Part 1 is diagnosis and Part 2 is demand, and why the chapter boundaries fall where they do. As rhetoric it works — the poem does structural work no outline could, and "Did you think that I, too, would yield?" earns its place. As a foundation for a derived book it's unusable, and you should not try to force it: a poem's stanzas and a rule system's dependency order are different shapes, and mapping one onto the other will produce chapters that are neither. Keep the manifesto as the manifesto. Its job is to make someone want the argument; it is not the argument.

The weakness is that Part 2 slides from demand into design — pods, councils, federation, funding streams, resilience funds — at a level of specificity that a manifesto can't support and doesn't need. Chapters 5 and 6 are book chapters wearing manifesto clothes.

## What "derived from nibli" can honestly mean

Not the strong reading. A logic program contains no arguments — no evidence, no causal theory, no empirical claims. Nothing in the KB entails that global agriculture produces 1.5× what we need, that Kenyan cash transfers didn't produce idleness, or that Aadhaar authentication failures killed Santoshi Kumari. Every persuasive fact in your existing book lives entirely outside the constitution and always will. If the new book claims its empirical content is derived, that's a false claim and a sharp reviewer will find it in an afternoon.

The honest and much stronger reading: the book's *normative architecture* is the KB exactly — the same commitments, the same consequences, the same refusals — and the book never asserts an entitlement the KB doesn't derive, never asserts a protection the KB doesn't hold under attack, and reports the consequences it didn't choose alongside the ones it did. Empirical claims are a visibly separate category, sourced the way your late chapters already source them.

What that buys is not reader-facing proof (the reader can't see the machine, so the rigor does no persuasive work directly) but three real things: internal consistency across 50,000 words, drift detection when you change your mind, and — the one that's actually a literary asset — **counterexamples you didn't invent to make your point**. Bela, Esa, Mira, Lupo, Ivo, Hano, Nia, Jala, Rebel, and now Don, Sly, Kel, Vex, Wren, Tyr, Ruk are already a cast. Each pin is a scene with a verdict that isn't the author's opinion. Don is the abuser who claimed whistleblower protection over his own victim and got it, until the rule changed. Vex is the auditor who was caught and kept the pen. Ruk is the violent man in the farmhouse. Those are stories ordinary readers understand instantly, and they arrive with an outcome the author cannot fudge.

## Recommendation

Write a third book, not a revision — and make its spine the dependency order of the constitution rather than a topic list.

That order is computable, and it gives you a thesis no other book in this genre can state: **your rights are unconditional and your punishments are appealable for the same structural reason, not because anyone promised.** The floor sits in the negation-free layer — it depends on nothing, so nothing can retract it, and no new fact about you can take it away. Sanctions sit in the layers below, all of them conditional on the absence of counter-evidence, which is exactly what makes them defeasible on appeal. That is a fact about how the rules are wired, not a value the author is asserting, and it is the single most valuable sentence you own. It's also the natural chapter one.

Then roughly one chapter per article, each opened by the person who broke it. Introduce every rule through its counterexample — the reader meets Don before they meet the shield clause. Show yourself being wrong in print; you've proven you can do it and it's the most credible register in the manuscript. State what the system cannot do as prominently as what it can: the fail-open window where a false accuser stays protected until adjudication is a real cost you chose deliberately, and saying so buys more trust than any amount of enthusiasm. Harvest the four strong late chapters more or less intact — they already read as derived, because they are.

Two consequences you should accept up front. The merit-points economics has to shrink hard, because the constitution supports only symbolic contribution and no arithmetic — engine float comparison is tolerant, which is disqualifying for anything that behaves like money. Either the chapter becomes qualitative or the KB grows exact integer arithmetic first, and that's engineering ahead of writing. And the tech chapters mostly go, or move to an appendix labelled as one possible implementation rather than the design.

Method for keeping the promise: a private claim-to-query table, one row per load-bearing sentence — the sentence, the query, the expected verdict. The reader never sees it. You regenerate it whenever the constitution changes and it tells you which paragraphs are now lying. This is the Catala pattern, and the farmhouse bug is the argument for it.

**Strongest alternative:** revise `book.md` in place, threading the derivation through the existing structure. It loses on arithmetic — the existing organization is topical and the derivation order isn't, so almost every chapter would need resequencing, and the third of the book with no constitutional support would need deleting anyway. You'd spend more effort demolishing than you'd spend building, and end up with something that reads as retrofitted, because it would be. Second alternative, splitting into two books, is worth considering only if the tech design has independent value to you; if it does, it's a separate short book and it should be.

**Risks.** The largest by far is mistaking derivability for justification — nothing in logic tells you the floor should contain expression and not water, and a formalization makes commitments precise, never justified. Say that in the introduction or the book is dishonest; saying it is also disarming. Beyond that: the KB gives zero protection against a wrong number, so the empirical claims need exactly the scrutiny your late chapters already apply; a hostile reviewer will read the published constitution against the book and find any drift, which is a reason to keep the mapping table rather than a reason to hide the repo; and the derivation discipline will slow drafting substantially, which matters given 42k words already exist.
