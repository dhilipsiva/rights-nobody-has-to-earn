**Status:** unsent.

**Attach:** nothing. The press sends its proposal form if a book fits.

**Before sending:** UCL Press's AI policy says generative AI "shouldn't replace
key authoring tasks (including producing original research and insights,
drawing conclusions and offering recommendations on a particular topic)". It
does not permit generative AI for figures, a term it defines to include
"computer code and formulas". This book falls on the wrong side of both, so
the message asks that question first. The press also charges £7,500 plus VAT
at this length unless the book wins one of five annual waivers for authors
outside UCL.

**Subject:** Outline and a question: *The Rights Nobody Has to Earn*

---

Dear Dr Penfold,

I would like to ask whether *The Rights Nobody Has to Earn: A worked design for
a society, with its formal claims made executable* could be considered for UCL
Press. Before you spend time on it, I also need to ask whether your AI policy
allows it.

The book is complete, at about 98,000 words. It asks what a society owes a
person who can offer it nothing in return. It answers with a constitution:
being a person gives standing and a floor of nine essentials, owed by named
public bodies, with no condition of work, wealth, citizenship or conduct. Each
chapter states a rule with cases and argues it against the strongest
alternative. Every rule is published as executable logic with public tests.

The book was written with AI assistance throughout. The design's rulings are
mine. An AI assistant drafted the prose, the arguments, the formal rules and
their tests, and I approved it. I have read your policy on generative AI and
authoring tasks, and on code and formulas, and I think the book falls outside
it as written. I would rather ask than submit something your policy excludes.
If the policy does admit a book made this way, I would welcome your proposal
form.

The manuscript is already public under CC BY 4.0, at
https://dhilipsiva.dev/rights-nobody-has-to-earn/. I am offering it to other
presses at the same time.
