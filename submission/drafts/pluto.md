Dear Pluto editors,

Please find below a proposal for *The Rights Nobody Has to Earn*, following your
guidelines.

**Title and subtitle.** *The Rights Nobody Has to Earn: A worked design for a
society, with its formal claims made executable*

**Rationale.**
1. It makes subsistence independent of employment, wealth, citizenship and
   approved conduct. It does so with a worked constitution, not a slogan: a
   floor of nine essentials owed by named public bodies to every person.
2. It argues every rule against its strongest alternative, with the evidence,
   the cost and who bears it. It opens each Part with a documented injustice:
   Jharkhand's cancelled ration cards, the Grootboom eviction, the Dutch
   childcare-benefits affair, *ADM Jabalpur* and the Bihar undertrials.
3. Every rule is published as executable logic with public tests, so readers
   can check the constitution's consequences for themselves rather than trust
   the author.

**Synopsis.** What does a society owe a person who can offer it nothing in
return, and how can that person hold it to the promise? The book opens on
Santoshi Kumari, eleven, who died in Jharkhand in 2017 after her family's
ration card was cancelled for not being linked to a biometric identity number.
It then tests a record that holds a child's birth and nothing else. Its answer
is a constitution. Being a person gives standing and a floor of nine
essentials, owed by named public bodies. Work, wealth, citizenship and good
conduct are not conditions. Each rule is stated plainly and argued against its
strongest alternative, with evidence and cost. Each is also published as
executable logic with public tests, so a reader can check what it entails. The
book claims its rules have the consequences it describes, not that the society
would work or is affordable.

**Market.** This is a book for the serious non-specialist reader who wants to

political theory and social policy.

**Review of competitive books.**
- John Rawls, *A Theory of Justice*, Belknap Press of Harvard University Press,
  1971; revised edition 1999. Rawls derives principles of justice; this book
  offers one concrete constitution through cases.
- Erik Olin Wright, *Envisioning Real Utopias*, Verso, 2010. Wright surveys
  alternatives to capitalism; this book specifies a single constitutional
  destination and leaves transition to a second volume.
- Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons*, Cambridge University Press, 1990.
  Ostrom analyses institutions empirically; this book is a normative design
  without equivalent field evidence.
