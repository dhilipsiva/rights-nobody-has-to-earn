# Chapter outline, a paragraph per chapter

This outline is for the presses that ask for contents with a paragraph per
chapter: Pluto, Polity and Verso. It is drawn from the book's
[annotated contents](../../book-1/reference.md#annotated-contents) and from
each chapter's closing argument. Every chapter states its rule with cases, then
argues it against the strongest alternative.

**Opening.** *A Note Before the Design* begins with Santoshi Kumari, eleven, who
died in Jharkhand in 2017 after her family's ration card was cancelled for not
being linked to a biometric identity number. The family's account and the
district's account of her death are both given. The note states the book's
question, its thesis, what it claims and what it does not, and how it was
written.

## Part I — Who counts, and what they are owed

*Opening case: The Sports Field at Wallacedene.* Families were evicted in the
Cape winter, and a judgment declined to define what everyone is owed.

1. **The Child With Nobody.** It shows what follows from a record that holds a
   child's birth and nothing else, and the other ways into the record, which
   need no papers, citizenship or family. It argues for standing before
   identity against identity-first welfare.
2. **What the Record May Say.** It covers which entries the rules accept, and
   what a temporary name given at a first encounter cannot prove. It argues for
   a record closed by name against the integrated case file, drawing on
   Robodebt, the Dutch childcare-benefits inquiry and SyRI.
3. **What You Are Owed.** It sets out the floor of nine essentials, and the
   difference between entitlement and fulfilment. It argues for a floor owed
   to everyone by named bearers, against a conditional duty and against a basic
   income.
4. **Whether It Arrived.** It covers evidence of delivery from the person
   served, with an independent witness. It argues for that evidence against
   verification at the counter.
5. **When There Is Genuinely Not Enough.** It covers the evidence and limits
   that govern a shortage, and what stays owed. It argues that a shortage must
   be proved and that need decides it, against triage that maximises benefit.
6. **Who Owes, and What Follows.** It covers public responsibility, continuity
   and remedy when an ordinary route fails, and follows one claim through every
   office that fails it. It argues for named bearers and a court that secures
   the minimum, against reasonableness review.

## Part II — The life the design leaves alone

*Opening case: Unprecedented Injustice.* Parents were branded as fraudsters by
rules with no room for a family's own case.

7. **An Ordinary Week.** It follows one adult's week of work, a home, a clinic,
   a school, a ballot, a dispute and a police stop, and shows how lightly the
   rules touch it. It argues, from freedom as non-domination, that power must
   write before it touches.
8. **What Nobody Has to Ask Permission For.** It covers learning, speech,
   belief and association, and the limits on public prescription. It argues
   for two closed grounds of restriction, with an end, against open
   proportionality.
9. **Work, Pay and Contribution.** It covers wages, supplements and competence
   without conditions on essentials, and records of contribution without a
   score. It argues for work above the floor, against conditionality based on
   reciprocity.
10. **What Money Cannot Buy.** It covers property, enterprise, exchange and
    public finance under constitutional limits. It argues for conditional
    ownership and lawful borrowing, against a debt brake.
11. **The Same Route for Everyone.** It covers discrimination, accommodation,
    justified distinctions and repair. It argues for discrimination judged by
    its effects and for positive measures that end, against equality blind to
    protected grounds.
12. **A Place in Which Life Remains Possible.** It covers the environmental
    right, ecological limits and future conditions. It argues for conditions
    kept on separate axes, against discounted cost-benefit analysis.
13. **Creatures Without a Ballot.** It covers animals' own interests, the uses
    that need grounds, and the orders that stop harm. It argues for animals as
    protected subjects with a claim of their own, against a welfare regime.
14. **Holding a Role in Somebody's Life.** It covers care, family, consent,
    supported agency and public continuity. It argues for support before
    substitution, against decisions made in a person's best interests.
15. **Arriving and Belonging.** It covers mobility, asylum and collective
    belonging without a price on personhood. It argues for membership by
    residence, against a state's right to exclude.

## Part III — The public power that serves it

*Opening case: The Brooding Spirit of the Law.* A court closed its doors to
detainees in an emergency, and an amendment reopened them.

16. **Answerability and Authority.** It separates permanent answerability,
    current office and lawful power. It argues for being answerable for good
    and empowered only for now, against a protective order that waits for a
    hearing.
17. **How Public Power Is Built.** It covers the federal republic's bodies, its
    separated functions and its routes through disagreement. It argues for a
    collective executive and a territorial chamber that can only delay, against
    Westminster government.
18. **The Vote Conviction Does Not Take.** It covers the vote and candidacy a
    conviction leaves in place, adulthood, and a person's political home. It
    argues for the vote outside the sentence, against disenfranchisement as
    punishment.
19. **What May Be Kept About You.** It covers records limited to their purpose,
    and separate permissions to watch, to compute and to decide. It argues for
    uses bound to one holding and decisions made by a person, against a state
    that asks to be told once.
20. **A Crisis Does Not Suspend the Republic.** It covers bounded emergency and
    external powers. It argues for four emergency powers with every right in
    force, against derogation.
21. **A Way to Be Heard.** It covers assistance, proceedings, remedies and
    enforcement, with no added power to imprison. It argues for separate stages
    of access, hearing and relief, with voluntary restoration beside
    adjudication, against a model centred on prosecution.
22. **Changing the Rules.** It covers political consent, certification of a
    candidate text, publication and the version in force. It argues for a fixed
    core behind an open route to amendment, against a fully revisable
    constitution.

## Part IV — What the design does to a person, and how it catches itself

*Opening case: Three to Ten Years.* Prisoners were held for years awaiting
trial for offences that might carry months, and a court found a speedy trial in
the right to life and liberty.

23. **The Shield.** It covers protection for people who expose authority, and
    the conditions under which an unrelated prosecution may proceed. It argues
    for protection that follows the charge, against a blanket immunity.
24. **Findings About People.** It covers credibility findings: whose signatures
    count, their grounds, review and limits. It also covers correcting a claim
    without condemning unrelated work. It argues for a restriction the size of
    the act, against disqualification and collateral consequences.
25. **A Prisoner Is a Person.** It treats custody as an independent backstop to
    personhood, never its price. It argues that a sentence takes liberty and
    nothing more, against suspended civil standing.
26. **Where People Are Put.** It covers placement and housing, and the
    difference between a recorded destination and an actual place. It argues
    that only proved harm opens the harshest place, against classification by
    offence or risk score.
27. **The One Thing Taken.** It covers the limits on custody, protective powers,
    and what actual release requires. It argues for a power to confine that
    expires, against abolition.
28. **When the System Notices It Broke.** It covers breach markers, the
    independent offices that must respond, and duties of individual and common
    repair. It argues for findings made from outside the walls, against
    internal inspection.

## Part V — The argument

*Opening case: After the Judgment.* It follows the Wallacedene families after
their judgment, including a litigant who died in her shack before her house was
built.

29. **The Five Joints.** It sets out the commitments beneath the choices and
    what the design adds. It shows how the choices fit together and trade off
    over valuation, rotation, coercion, capture and the state.
30. **Where This Could Fail.** It ranks the failures that cross chapters by an
    argued order. For each it gives the evidence that would show it, what the
    design does now, and what a fix would take.

**Back matter.** It comprises an optional method that explains how the rules
are run and tested, the works cited, and a map, glossary and index with five
diagrams. A separate summary edition prints the constitution as thirty-one
numbered articles in plain language.
