Dear `[AUTHOR: editor's name]`,

I am writing to ask whether you would consider a proposal for *The Rights
Nobody Has to Earn: A worked design for a society, with its formal claims made
executable*, a complete manuscript of about 98,000 words.

The book asks what a society owes a person who can offer it nothing in return,
and how that person can hold it to the promise. It answers with a constitution.
Being a person gives standing and a floor of nine essentials, owed by named
public bodies. Work, wealth, citizenship and good conduct are not conditions.
Each chapter states one rule plainly, with cases, then argues it against the
strongest alternative: the evidence, the cost and who bears it, and what would
change the choice.

What may interest the Press is that every rule is published as executable
logic with public tests. A reader can check what the constitution entails for a
named person, and a companion runs every chapter's cases in the browser. The
book claims that its rules have the consequences it describes, and argues its
choices. It does not claim that the society would work or is affordable.
