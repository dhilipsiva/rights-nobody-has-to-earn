## 1. Ratified mandate

The settled Book 1 has a narrow, verified kernel: equal standing, named floor
entitlements, limited consequences, amendment, audit, and a staged temporal path
for record carry, event order, and case-bound custody authority. It deliberately
defers governance vocabulary, most **non-carceral** delivery routes, and most
operational accountability to Book 2. It is therefore **not currently a
comprehensive constitution**.

The author has chosen to expand Book 1 into a formal constitutional interface
for the domains below. The choice changes **scope**, not the current truth of
any claim: the present constitution remains narrow until new rule families are
formalised and verified. The expansion requires a new constitution, regenerated
spine, pins, counterfactuals, and an explicit Book 1/Part V/Book 2 boundary. It
is not an additive prose pass.

## 2. Boundary rule

Book 1 specifies **who holds a claim or power; what must be protected,
provided, prohibited, recorded, remedied, and reviewed**. Book 2 specifies
**how it is staffed, funded, built, measured, transitioned, or operated**.

| Keep in Book 1 | Keep in Book 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum guarantee, duty-bearer, evidence, limit, remedy, appeal, independence | Tax rates, budgets, staffing, procurement, facilities, service workflows |
| Constitutional institutions and separation of functions | Appointment administration, salaries, office systems, training |
| A firewall for operational assessments and data | Databases, cryptography, devices, assessment tools, security practice |
| Non-derogable emergency limits and review | Incident command, stockpiles, disaster logistics |
| Ecological/non-regression constraints | Models, allocations, land-use and emissions programmes |

The boundary is epistemic, not evasive: Book 1 makes a claim testable as a
constitutional invariant; Book 2 must support its real-world claims with
evidence, costs, data, and reproducible analysis.

### Required recursive paths

## 6. Democratic corridor

**Status: ratified constitutional design rule; formal contract pending.** A
majority chooses among rights-compatible options.
It does not vote on whether a person counts, whether basic protection applies,

The collective/plurality settlement distinguishes an internal collective choice
from a second general-government vote. Authenticated actual collective consent
is required for permanent forced relocation, extinguishment or irreversible
impairment of collective title, sacred-site destruction, hazardous-material
placement, and comparable existential harms. Other material effects require

voice, appeal, and Constitutional Court review. Collective autonomy adds neither

| Pollution is cumulative, cross-regional, outsourced, or left by an insolvent actor | Common competence where effects cross regions; prevention and cessation without a fault prerequisite; strict containment, restoration, and reasonable response-cost liability for an inherently hazardous activity after its causal connection is established; otherwise adjudicated causal contribution or control; immediate public restoration for orphan harm; later cost recovery; anti-evasion and non-export controls; separately adjudicated individual culpability for personal punishment; and a remedy that does not punish workers or floor recipients. |
| A person claims an ecological right as a material-floor outcome | Refuse the category error. Clean, healthy, sustainable environmental protection requires liberty, equality, public-duty, record, process, and commons cards; it is not a new delivery predicate certifying a successful environment or personal state. |
| A human controls, uses, confines, transports, or kills an animal | The applicable Class-10 welfare minimum; sentience evidence and uncertainty; prohibited suffering; the alternative-sensitive rule for any controlled food-production use; the categorical refusal of unrelieved severe or prolonged research suffering; the enhanced test for every lethal, invasive, or high-severity use; species-appropriate care; inspection, advocate, challenge, rescue or cessation, and remedy. Ownership or culture is no severe-cruelty exemption. |
| Wild animals face habitat loss, invasive-species control, disease, predation, or other natural suffering | Separate individual-welfare and Class-9 species/habitat effects; direct duties for human-caused or controllable harm; evidence, necessity, proportionality, least suffering, ecological compatibility, review, and remedy. The constitution does not promise to eliminate predation, disease, or all natural suffering. |
| A disaster is declared | Trigger, authorised bodies, non-derogable protections, reasons, challenge, an adjudicated reversal/end condition, and explicit temporal status. The declaration confers only procedural acceleration, resource redirection, compensated requisition, and hazard-specific reviewable restriction; it suspends nothing. |
| An emergency is never ended, or its renewal record is frozen | No current renewal evidence means no current legal effect; the floor and essential-service continuity survive the lapse; restoration, post-hoc audit and remedy on end. State that fail-safe polarity defeats a renewal never made but **not** a frozen or replayed record, and that Book 1 cannot make an emergency end. |
| The authorising body cannot convene | A predeclared alternate authorising route and a predeclared independent substitute reviewer, bound by identical limits and ratified at the ordinary body's first opportunity. Neither absence becomes approval nor an indefinite hold; the capture risk of the alternate is stated rather than hidden. |
| An officer uses force and an unarmed person dies | Lawful trigger; strict necessity and unavoidability; burden of lawfulness on the state; investigation by a body other than the deploying one; family participation; personal accountability unabsorbed by rank; public findings; remedy. |
| An order is manifestly unlawful | The duty to refuse; protection for the refuser; the absence of an orders defence; command responsibility upward. |
| A person is arrested or held before trial | Individualised recorded ground; identification and reasons; notification of a chosen person; counsel, interpreter and accommodation; prompt **automatic** independent judicial review; the complete floor, voice, franchise and candidacy retained. |
| A person is restricted without conviction — quarantine, exclusion, border hold | A separate instrument with its own trigger, evidence, review and temporal contract; no punitive consequence; no feeding of severity, placement, conviction or recognition; no risk or dangerousness assessment; and shelter plus a recorded voice wherever the state physically holds the person. |
| Surveillance is proposed at population scale | Refusal of bulk and suspicionless collection; individualised prior judicial authorisation; least intrusive means; defined duration; later notification; challenge and remedy; no product entering the canonical consequential person record. |
| An unaccompanied child arrives at a frontier | No immigration detention; independent standing and voice; best interests as a primary consideration; the floor immediately; determination with an advocate and interpreter; absolute non-refoulement. |
| A resident is ordered expelled, or another state requests a surrender | Individual reasoned decision; suspensive appeal; family life and children considered; no collective expulsion; no statelessness; the non-refoulement and categorical-refusal bars, with a diplomatic assurance treated as weak evidence rather than a cure. |
| A hospital, school or court is asked for immigration data | The enforcement firewall covering **enrolment** as well as collection and transmission; no conditioning of access; no adverse inference from an absent record. |
| Compulsory service is enacted and a person objects | Unconditional objection to a lethal or armed role; no sincerity tribunal; a genuinely equivalent non-punitive alternative; no loss of floor, standing, franchise, candidacy, education or employment; no repeated punishment for the same objection. |
| An armed attack occurs while the legislature cannot sit | An immediate defensive response permitted, submitted for ratification without delay and ending absent it; the predeclared alternate authorising route; no aggressive or secret war; evidenced attribution where the attack is cyber or infrastructural. |
| A treaty or arbitration clause would lower a protected baseline | The supremacy limit and the ratification/review route — stated as a rule addressed to ratifiers, reviewers and courts, with the express acknowledgement that no current mechanism reads a treaty's actual effect. |
| A region completes a lawful exit | The already ratified settlement contents as a floor — borders, assets, debts, commons, floors, and minority protections — plus continuing protection for people losing membership; actual Indigenous collective consent where title or sovereignty over collective lands is affected; no military instrument, no weaponised trade, border or service levers, and no defence mandate over the exited territory; recognition as a named external assumption. |
