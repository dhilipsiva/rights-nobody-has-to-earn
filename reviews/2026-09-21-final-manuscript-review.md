# Final manuscript review — 21 September 2026

My assessment is **9/10 as a manuscript**: a distinctive, substantially
defended constitutional proposal with unusually inspectable consequences.
The strongest writing makes a hard distinction concrete through a person's
case. The remaining weakness is density: some chapters still ask readers to
retain too many institutional conditions before reaching the consequence.
The book merits serious editorial consideration. It does not earn a perfect
score merely because its revision tracker closes.

This review freshly read all 34 inputs in manifest order: the epigraph,
opening, chapters 1–31 and optional method, starting at `ee588deb`. It includes
the shield correction described below. Source inspection concentrated on
consequential claims and their existing cases; it was not a line-by-line
reading of the entire constitution or every fixture. Complete execution is
reported separately. This is an AI-assisted assessment within the project,
not an independent endorsement, reader study or observation of a working
society. The revised canonical prose is `session-drafted, author-approved
under delegated approval (2026-09-13)`.

The book's strongest contribution is the conjunction of an intelligible
constitutional proposal, recurring cases and executable consequences. It
does not need to claim that unconditional rights, independent review or
formal reasoning about law are new inventions. The child/prisoner pairing
gives the proposal a memorable test: the same standing survives both the
absence of achievement and adjudicated wrongdoing.

## Material finding and correction

Chapter 24 said at several points that the shield blocked a conviction;
the Part V comparison and method paragraph also overstated its reach into
prosecution. The current source blocks **confinement under the conviction**.
Both Article 6 routes conclude `prisoner(subject, case)` and require an
already recorded conviction. The separate recorded-conviction rule has no
shield guard. In the [existing Rex sequence](../book-1/24-the-shield.pins.nibli),
`match(Rex, ConvictionRecorded)` is true while the shield keeps custody false.
Qualified unrelatedness permits confinement, and a later established reviewer
conflict blocks it again.

This was a material overclaim in the reader projection, not a new failure of
the formal rule. Chapter 24, the corresponding Part V passage and the method
now state the narrower consequence explicitly. They preserve the conviction
record, separate protective powers, review requirements and the injured
person's claims. The existing coverage entry and generated report are aligned.
The [shield decision](../book-1/appendix/decisions/shield-scope-decision.md)
records the correction; the exact approved text remains in the chapters.
No rule or executable expectation was weakened to obtain a pass.

## Adversarial rechecks

The review revisited the following questions against current prose, selected
rules, substantive cases and controlling decisions. These dispositions do not
claim that every conceivable constitutional objection has been resolved.

| Question | Supported disposition |
|---|---|
| Does a credibility finding still withdraw recognition for unrelated contribution? | The earlier defect is removed. The current [recognition decision](../book-1/appendix/decisions/recognition-purpose-decision.md) and [live cases](../tests/pins/credibility/recognition-removal/expect.pins.nibli) preserve care, wages, supplements and standing without a constitutional badge or blanket clawback. New adverse signatures remain restricted; review is owed without a request, and a qualified disposition supplies restoration. The prose does not invent automatic expiry or completed review. |
| Does severity force secure placement or follow from a victim count? | Neither claim describes the current rule. The [boundary cases](../tests/pins/custody/severity-boundary/expect.pins.nibli) distinguish grave injury or aggravated cruelty from ordinary injuries to multiple people. Severe cases can retain home or supported residence. Secure placement separately needs necessity and insufficient less restrictive alternatives; missing review or mismatched case evidence withholds it. |
| Can a scarce resource be allocated by an arbitrary lottery or an unsupported declaration of priority? | The current comparison requires relevant grounds, reasons and compatible allocation methods. The [unequal-claims case](../tests/pins/scarcity/worked/lottery-for-unequal-claims/expect.pins.nibli) refuses the lottery; a [materially equal case](../tests/pins/scarcity/worked/materially-equal-lottery-continuity/expect.pins.nibli) permits it. A [comparison defect](../tests/pins/scarcity/worked/challenge-and-comparison-defect/expect.pins.nibli) withdraws the allocation while the genuine scarcity and separate shortfall duties remain. No numerical optimiser or uniquely correct weighting is claimed. |
| Is delegated discretion merely hidden behind signatures? | The book identifies substantive grounds as well as separated functions: merits, individual necessity, comparative need, rejected alternatives and challenge. These judgments still enter as supplied findings. Chapters 3, 6, 17, 24 and 28 and Part V expose that dependence. Agreement is not presented as truth, appointment as actual independence, or form completion as justification. |
| Can disclosures remove every shield reviewer, or can the prosecutor qualify itself? | The current Chapter 24 sequence and shield-independence cases distinguish an allegation from an authorised conflict finding. Positive case-specific qualification is required; fused functions and parties cannot decide their own independence. Actual conflict defeats a favourable certificate. The old disclosure veto remains an explicit counterfactual, not the enacted rule. |
| Does becoming publicly answerable destroy an individual's birth-based standing? | This suspicion is disproved by the source distinction. Seating derives `authority`, while the birth rule excludes the separate `public` institution entry. Choosing a person for office does not derive that institutional entry. Chapters 2 and 16 correctly separate the two meanings. |
| Does the book mistake institutional capture controls for capture prevention in fact? | The current argument acknowledges that nominally separate selectors can share interests, that appointments can fail, and that published records can be false. Its comparative reasons concern bounded powers, contestability and the cost of retaliation. It makes no empirical claim that the specified machinery defeats every coalition or produces willing institutions. |
| Do successful formal checks establish a justified design or a working society? | The opening, Part V and method consistently deny that inference. Protected claims, source identity, permissions and duties are separately argued and tested; staffing, authentic evidence, resources and performance remain unproved. A constitutional gap would still belong to Book 1, rather than being closed by assigning operation to Book 2. |

No additional material defect was established by these rechecks. That is a
bounded review result, not a proof of completeness. The shield overclaim was
repaired; the suspected standing conflict was disproved; the earlier
recognition, severity and scarcity repairs remain supported. No newly found
design failure is being closed here merely as an unavoidable trade-off.

## Evidence and remaining editorial limits

The fresh read incorporates item 31's [recorded evidence review](../book-1/appendix/decisions/narrative-register-decision.md#evidence-and-inference-review--item-31-2026-09-21).
It does not claim another retrieval of every external source. That work
withdrew an unsupported cancellation count, narrowed the Auroville holding,
supplied the contemporaneous source for the disputed Santoshi account, and
removed an unreproduced democracy-index comparison. The bundled V-Dem
calculation was reproduced; observational association is not made a causal
case for this constitution. Several retained study descriptions are checked
against published abstracts, and the background monographs were not freshly
read in full. Those limits remain explicit in the existing records.

Three qualities keep the manuscript below 10/10. First, the vocabulary of
qualified findings and separate duties accumulates despite the improved case
structure. Second, Part V must carry much of the comparison and justification;
the opening links make it accessible earlier but do not remove the weight of
the final chapter. Third, the historical examples illuminate institutional
questions more convincingly than they predict the behaviour of this proposed
combination. More confident rhetoric would not repair that evidential limit.

The most promising further editorial polish would shorten repeated sequences
of role qualifications where the consequence is already clear and sharpen
the transitions within Part V's State argument. Those are matters of pace and
emphasis, not hidden unresolved failures in the cases above. The current book
remains complete enough to be judged as a manuscript on its own terms.
