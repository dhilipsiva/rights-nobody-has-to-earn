# Revision review — 25 September 2026

This review reads the revised manuscript fresh, after the revision tracker's
items 34–64: the D1–D9 rulings, the restructure, the argument sections, the
Part openers, the rewritten opening, method and Part V, the companion's
articles and chapter cases, and production. It starts from `66406f16` and
describes the text as corrected in the commit that records it. It protects
neither the September 20 nor the September 21 review's diagnosis.

All 40 ordered inputs were read in sequence: the epigraph, the opening note,
the five Part openers, Chapters 1–30, the method, the works cited and the map,
glossary and index. Five reading passes, one for each Part with its opener
and one for Part V and the back matter, each read its inputs in order and
checked suspicious claims against the paired pins, the constitution, the
registry and the measurement records. Every material finding was then checked
against the text and the formal source before anything changed; findings that
did not survive that check were set aside. The passes and this synthesis are
AI-assisted work within the same project. They are an editorial judgment, not
an independent review, a reader study or an endorsement.

## Overall

**Manuscript: 8/10. Publisher readiness: 7.5/10.**

The book now argues. Each chapter states its rule flatly and then defends it
against the strongest alternative, with the evidence, the cost and who bears
it, and Part V draws the choices together and ranks where the design could
fail. The documented cases that open the Parts are the book's strongest new
material: each is exact about what the record shows and restrained about what
it does not. The claim discipline holds across 98,000 words. Derived text
states duties and conclusions, not events; the arguments state their sources'
limits beside their figures.

The reading passes found about forty precision errors, and every one that
survived checking has been corrected (below). Most were small: an arrival verb
where the design concludes a duty, a pronoun the one-line record cannot
support, a sentence that said more than its pins. Three were serious: Chapter
25 said a release entry gives free movement, which Chapter 27's pins
contradict; Part V presented custody's lapse as the answer to undertrial
detention without saying that a lapse releases nobody; and re-examining a wage
finding showed that a compensation attestation named no payer, so a fraud
finding on its basis could owe restitution to an employer it never concerned.
All three are fixed, the last in the rules. What keeps the
rating at 8 rather than higher is density and repetition rather than error:
several derived chapters read as catalogues (6, 13, 17, 27), the argument
sections often restate their chapter's cost paragraph, and the book is long
for its primary reader.

## Material findings and corrections

Each finding below was confirmed against the text, its pins or the source
before it was corrected. The exact revised prose is `session-drafted,
author-approved under delegated approval (2026-09-13)`.

**Statements the formal source contradicts.**

- Chapter 25 said a release entry gives the named person free movement.
  Chapter 27's pins keep Hano held after a release entry: the entry gives
  standing, which brings free movement only where no custody case or restraint
  holds the person. Corrected.
- Part V offered custody's lapse as the answer to the undertrial detention of
  Part IV's case. The lapse ends lawful authority and releases nobody; release
  is a further act, a person still held is owed humane care and review of the
  holding, and detention before trial has its own prompt, automatic judicial
  review. Corrected.
- Chapter 21 said Ruk waits for an owed hearing; Ruk is owed a hearing only
  once a request is made. Chapter 24 implied that a record of raw relief entries
  alone concludes custody. Chapter 10 read the political-finance bar as binding
  associations; the pinned bar is an enterprise relabelling itself. Chapter 9
  said a certificate or wage "passes to no child", which its pins do not say
  and inheritance contradicts. Chapter 14 continued an adult status Esa never
  held. Chapter 17's table gave certification to the electoral administration,
  which its own text gives to a separate result service. Chapter 25 listed the
  roots of standing as though complete. All corrected.

**Overclaims and arrival language.** Findings that "reach" offices (Chapter 6),
repair that "proceeds" or "goes ahead" (Chapters 12 and 14), an accused who
"always meets" a contestable case and a copy that "proves" a filing (Chapter
21), an Assembly that "always" can sit (Chapter 20), protection "running"
during a wait (Chapter 30) and "any encounter" giving standing (Chapter 30)
now state duties, conditions and recorded encounters. Chapter 13 twice dropped
"solely" from the animal core's ban on dispensable killing; restored.

**Sources.** The Mandela Rules' Rule 3 carries an exception the paraphrase
dropped; Chapter 18 now distinguishes *Sauvé*, which left open only
restrictions on standing for office, from *Hirst* and *NICRO*; Wallacedene lay
on the edge of Oostenberg, as the judgment says; Santoshi Kumari's family's
card was cancelled for not being linked, not for a failed match; Part V's
Gelman and Stern sentence no longer implies a second estimate the reader was
not shown. The registry binding for the Mandela Rules follows the corrected
wording.

**Consistency and presentation.** Nell has no pronoun anywhere, as in Chapter
1: the one-line record holds only a birth. Chapter 4 no longer repeats Chapter
1's Jharkhand figures; Chapters 6, 20 and 28 no longer re-tell passages other
chapters own. Chapter 11's sentence about a recognition status that does not
exist is gone. Chapter 16 names the common tier consistently; the reference's
cast list names only the people who recur; the glossary's shield entry gives
its dating limit and material security its delivery evidence. Twelve smaller
sentences were repaired for grammar or clarity. The method's belief-rule,
contradiction-test and constraint sentences now say exactly what the
repository does.

**Back matter.** The works cited had dropped works cited in a note's later
sentences (Podmore, Harrison, Leviatan, Cinelli, Forney and Pearl,
*DeGraffenreid*, the Hungarian Fundamental Law) and mis-sorted a few entries;
the generator now reads every sentence, trims trailing commentary and sorts
by the author a possessive or a parenthesis names. The index had false
positives (a recollection indexed as "recall", Germany's court as the
republic's, language interpretation as the interpretation article) and
missing places and people; its terms were narrowed, its chapter-title rule
added and eight people added.

**Formal.** Chapter 8 says all ten freedom limits hold for Nell, and its pins
tested nine; the tenth is now pinned.

A reader asked whether Part V was right to call a wage the employer alone
attests a failure of the design. Checking the rule behind it found a defect
the question had not named: the compensation attestation recorded the kind,
the person and the basis but not the payer. One attester's wage record for a
person therefore concluded the wage of every payer who had promised that
person one, and a Court fraud finding on that basis concluded restitution to
each of those payers, including one the attested basis never concerned. The
attester now records the payer on the same basis as the kind, in the
compensation and restitution rules alike. A regression in the family's pins
failed four ways on the earlier rule and passes on the repaired one; Chapter
9's pins carry a second payer, and every wage fixture records its payer. The
refusal of the payer's own attestation stays: it is the rule that no party
certifies itself. The recipient, authorised to attest their own pay, may be
the attester. Part V now calls the wage the nearest exception to procedure
falling on the party who acts, and Chapter 30 gives, as the sign of failure, a
worker whose pay nobody else is authorised to attest. The compensation
contract card records the repair and the alternatives compared.
